# Consolidate help content without losing an answer

Deliver a smaller or more coherent set of actual article drafts and a traceable account of what each existing article becomes. The objective is to remove conflicting or fragmented explanations while preserving the answers readers still need. Fewer articles is not itself success, and a proposed folder tree is not a completed content consolidation.

[Site navigation](../author-site-navigation/SKILL.md) arranges routes through established pages. [Classification design](../design-classification-scheme/SKILL.md) defines categories. [Document revision reconciliation](../document-revision-reconciliation/SKILL.md) merges versions of one document. This workflow instead reconciles the substantive coverage of several independently useful articles. Use [technical-guide writing](../write-technical-guide/SKILL.md) when the missing work is establishing a new procedure rather than preserving and restructuring existing answers.

## Establish the bounded content contract

Identify the selected collection and article revisions, intended reader tasks, approved audiences, product/version/plan/locale distinctions, authoritative behavior or policy sources, and the requested output format. Establish whether the task is a comparison or includes replacement drafts; an ordinary request to consolidate the articles includes drafts in the requested private destination, not live publication.

Read the articles themselves, including warnings, tables, screenshots that carry instructions, attachments and linked prerequisites needed to interpret them. Search results and titles identify candidates but do not establish equivalent content. Keep inaccessible portions visible. Use existing authorized sources; do not crawl an entire organization or collect support conversations merely to make the inventory look complete.

Ask only about a missing decision that changes the consolidation: for example, whether both legacy and current product versions remain supported. Preserve existing behavior, policy, entitlement and safety wording until its governing source supports a change. A newer edit timestamp, greater traffic or repeated wording does not establish which conflicting answer is correct.

## 1. Inventory article identity and material coverage

Give each article a stable ID, source revision/capture, exact locator, title, audience, applicable variants and current publication state. Keep article identity separate from URL and title. Two translated pages or two articles with the same heading are not automatically duplicates.

Extract material content units at a useful level: a complete answer, prerequisite, conditional step, warning, exception, limitation or completion check. Do not create one unit for every sentence. For each unit retain its source article/section and the conditions under which it is true. Preserve negation, ordering and dependencies; a warning before an operation is not interchangeable with a footnote after it.

Record whether an apparent gap is absent from inspected content, inaccessible, outside the selected collection or unresolved. If support/search evidence is supplied, use it to identify relevant reader tasks while preserving its sample and time window. Low page views do not establish that a rare but necessary answer is obsolete.

## 2. Separate redundancy from real variants

Compare candidate units using their reader task, starting state, audience, applicable product/version, required action and promised outcome. Similar text can conceal different conditions. Different wording can describe the same supported answer.

Choose a justified treatment:

- Equivalent units under the same conditions can share one maintained answer, retaining all source lineage
- Common material plus a real variant can use a shared introduction with clearly labeled branches, or separate articles when a combined page would obscure eligibility
- One article covering unrelated tasks can split into independently usable drafts, each carrying the prerequisites and warnings it needs
- Complementary fragments can combine only when the resulting sequence is supported; do not splice incompatible procedures into a new unverified workflow
- Conflicting claims remain held with their exact sources and the decision needed; do not average numbers, pick the shortest wording or silently prefer the apparent majority

Distinguish stale source material from content that is still valid for a narrower audience. Retirement requires evidence that its necessary answers are preserved or an authorized decision to remove that coverage. Keep translations and localized requirements separate unless their equivalence is actually established.

## 3. Map every material unit to its disposition

Build a compact many-to-many coverage map with:

`source article/revision and unit; applicable conditions; disposition; target draft/section; preservation or change basis; unresolved decision`

Useful dispositions are retained, combined as equivalent, moved, split across named targets, intentionally excluded under a stated decision, or held. A source article can feed several targets, and a target can combine several sources. Preserve that relationship rather than forcing a one-old-page/one-new-page mapping.

For combined duplicates, record the retained unit and all equivalent source locators. For a split, make clear which target carries each necessary part and whether a prerequisite remains a linked dependency. Reconcile all in-scope articles and all material units, including held and excluded material. Article counts and content-unit counts have different meanings; neither substitutes for preservation review.

An article-level disposition follows its units. Do not mark an article ready to retire while a unique required unit is held, a destination is incomplete, or an applicable audience would lose access. Keep a candidate retirement recommendation separate from permission to unpublish or delete anything.

## 4. Write the replacement articles

Produce the actual requested draft set from the resolved map. Each draft needs a clear reader task, applicability, prerequisites, the supported answer or procedure, material exceptions and an observable completion condition when the source provides one. Keep exact controls and version labels tied to their source; do not invent a menu path to smooth a gap.

Avoid duplicated bodies that will drift again. Use one maintained explanation with intentional cross-links where suitable, while keeping each entry point understandable. Link to the specific destination section rather than making a reader rediscover an answer in a large generic article. Do not collapse distinct conditions into vague phrases such as “depending on your plan.” State the known branches or hold the missing rule.

Keep editorial uncertainty out of final-sounding reader instructions. Isolate an incomplete draft or mark the specific section as awaiting review in the private candidate. Maintain an editor-only decision record when comments, internal procedures or unpublished details are unsuitable for the intended audience. Public drafts must not expose restricted article titles, internal escalation routes, customer examples, tokens or private source URLs through comments, links or metadata.

## 5. Prepare continuity and retirement gates

For each proposed replacement or retirement, identify known dependencies: inbound links, help links inside the product, saved replies, embedded articles, attachments and translated counterparts. Inspect only the authorized dependency sources and label incomplete backlink coverage. A successful text search is not proof that no external links exist.

Record the target's readiness, audience/access equivalence, reviewer or owner when supplied, old-link treatment and any remaining dependency hold. A restricted replacement cannot silently take over a public answer. Do not publish a broader-access copy of internal material to make the mapping convenient.

Use [URL redirect preparation](../prepare-url-redirect-map/SKILL.md) for actual old-to-new routing work when requested. The content map alone does not install redirects, preserve fragment destinations or update product links. This workflow ends with drafts and an actionable transition record unless a separate authorized publication task is established.

## Acceptance and recovery

Read the exact saved drafts and map back. Check:

1. Every selected article and material unit has a supported disposition; nothing vanished merely because it was awkward to merge
2. Each target section traces to supporting source units, and every intentional change has a stated basis
3. Version, role, plan, locale, warning and prerequisite distinctions survived in the reader-facing path
4. A few concrete reader tasks reach a complete answer for each consequential variant, with blocked cases honestly visible
5. All emitted links point to intended existing or clearly identified draft targets; proposed destinations are not presented as published URLs
6. Retirement-ready candidates have no unresolved unique coverage or access dependency, and privacy review includes both reader content and hidden/editorial material

These are content and structural checks. Do not claim measured findability, reduced contacts or successful customer use without separate evidence.

If a source changes, compare the affected units against the pinned revision and update only dependent drafts and retirement decisions. Preserve unrelated concurrent edits. If a save is uncertain, inspect the destination before retrying; retain a separate candidate when an update would overwrite unreviewed work. If an unsupported merge loses an exception, restore that unit from the source and recheck every target that depended on the combined answer.

Deliver the replacement drafts, coverage map, unresolved decisions and transition/retirement holds in proportion to the collection. A short set can fit in one document. Finish when the requested candidate is usable and its remaining decisions are explicit; publication, deletion, sending support replies and measuring live outcomes are separate actions.

## Fictional illustration, not executed

A fictional help center has three articles. A1 explains exporting the current workspace in product version 4. A2 repeats that answer and adds the requirement that an administrator enable exports first. A3 explains version 3, where the supplied source says the administrator must perform the export themselves.

The proposed current-version draft combines A1 and A2, preserving the enablement prerequisite before the export steps. The legacy draft retains A3's different actor requirement. The map records two sources feeding the current draft and one feeding the legacy draft; it does not convert the legacy answer into a universal instruction.

A product help button still points to A2. A2 is only a retirement candidate until the replacement and that entry path are ready. If the administrator-enablement rule has no authoritative version-4 source, its unit and A2's retirement remain held rather than disappearing from the shortened article.

All names and behavior above are invented. No article was published, retired, redirected, used by a reader or measured for support effectiveness.
