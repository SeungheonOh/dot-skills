# Prepare the speaker's route through an existing presentation

Deliver notes the speaker can use while presenting: what to say at each meaningful point, what the audience should notice, how to move between points and what to cut if time shrinks. Tie these cues to the exact talk/deck revision and the actual presentation setup. A transcript of every slide or a generic public-speaking checklist is not enough.

Use [source-backed-presentation](../source-backed-presentation/SKILL.md) to create or substantively revise a deck, [write-original-content](../write-original-content/SKILL.md) for a new speech, and [prepare-product-demo](../prepare-product-demo/SKILL.md) for an action-and-result product walkthrough. This workflow prepares delivery of already established material; it does not need to rebuild its content or design.

## Establish the talk and the speaker's constraints

Read the full selected talk/deck, including relevant notes, appendix material and media. Identify the intended audience, occasion, purpose, exact duration if known, whether that includes introductions/questions, and requested delivery format. Preserve required wording, disclosure statements, claims, audience decisions and the speaker's actual position.

Find the few practical details that change the notes: live room versus remote/hybrid, available display/device, presenter application/version when known, co-presenter boundaries, media playback, caption/interpreter arrangements supplied by the user, and preferred cue density. A new speaker may want near-verbatim opening and closing lines; an experienced speaker may prefer brief prompts. Do not diagnose confidence or infer speech needs from personal characteristics.

Ask only about a gap that changes the route, such as whether a ten-minute slot includes questions. If the delivery setup is unknown, finish the content-linked cues and label the technology handoff provisional. Do not invent an event duration, hardware availability, organizer agreement or permission to join the actual meeting.

## Bind spoken cues to stable content anchors

Use the deck revision plus slide ID/title or a stable section anchor, not page numbers alone. Record the displayed slide number as a convenience. A later insert can shift every number without changing the subject; a changed slide title or content can invalidate a cue even if its number stays the same.

For each meaningful segment, prepare only the detail the speaker needs:

- Entry cue and central takeaway
- Short spoken explanation that adds context instead of reading the whole slide
- Exact visual, value, relationship or passage to direct attention toward
- Required qualification or wording that must travel with the claim
- Transition to the next segment and any supported media/control cue
- Estimated duration, optional expansion and safe cut destination when useful

Write complete transition and recovery lines where wording matters. Keep factual explanations grounded in the selected material. Do not invent a personal anecdote, audience reaction, endorsement, result, promise or belief for the speaker. Preserve the distinction between findings, interpretation and a proposal. If a necessary claim lacks support, mark it for content correction rather than strengthening it through confident delivery language.

Describe important visual information in spoken terms when it is necessary to follow the argument. For a chart, identify the relevant categories, direction, units and caveat; do not instruct the speaker merely to say “as you can see.” Keep any exact number consistent with the selected slide. Do not promise accessibility certification from a narration pass.

Keep private coaching and source concerns separate from audience-ready prose. Notes inside a shared deck are part of that file. Do not insert private Q&A strategy, confidential background or another person's personal details into native speaker notes unless that destination and its audience permit them.

## Build timing routes that preserve meaning

Allocate the known slot across the actual introduction, core explanation, closing and requested questions. Count media runtime, slide transitions, audience interaction and co-presenter handoff when they consume the slot. State assumptions for unknown durations rather than treating them as zero.

Use supplied rehearsal measurements where available. Otherwise label all timing as estimates. A word-count estimate does not establish a performed runtime or include audience interruptions. Do not replace missing measurements with invented observations.

Prepare an essential route and optional material when the time constraint warrants it. Each cut must name the content omitted, the destination anchor and the bridge line. Check that the shortened route still introduces terms before using them and retains evidence, limitations and the closing point. If removing an example leaves a later “as we just saw” unsupported, revise that transition. Never cut the caveat while retaining the claim it limits.

Give cumulative checkpoints rather than a brittle sentence-by-sentence stopwatch. If the talk is behind at a checkpoint, identify the next coherent cut. When even the essential route exceeds the slot, show the actual scope decision instead of making every segment implausibly short. Preserve a separate full version; cutting delivery does not authorize deleting slides from the source.

## Make the technology handoff specific to the setup

Identify the exact presentation file/revision, required media, viewing application, expected slide-show surface, private-note surface, control method and any named operator. Distinguish a configuration plan from an observed setup. A file opening on one computer does not prove fonts, audio, animations, presenter controls or links will work on the actual device.

Use current official guidance for version-specific behavior. [Microsoft's Presenter View guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/training/start-the-presentation-and-see-your-notes-in-presenter-view) explains the separation between a notes view and an audience slide view. That feature's existence does not prove the right monitor, window or meeting-share surface has been selected. The acceptance check is what the audience-facing surface actually shows in an authorized check.

Prepare a short setup handoff that names the checks needed for this talk: readable notes, correct first/next slide, intended audience view, required media output, supplied accessibility arrangements and the agreed ending surface. Do not join a live meeting, share a screen, record participants, alter display/privacy settings or grant permissions merely to complete preparation. Use already authorized checks in a suitable private context; otherwise leave their status pending.

Inspect proposed screenshots, notes, fallback copies and shared surfaces for unrelated tabs, messages, recent-file lists, personal paths and hidden material. A cropped window is only a proposed boundary until its actual shared output is checked. Do not broaden file permissions to make media or a link work.

For a fallback, identify the actual available file and what it preserves. A static PDF can preserve visible slides while losing animation, notes and embedded playback. Explain that tradeoff at the affected cue. Do not call an unavailable backup ready or substitute a still image while claiming a live animation was shown. If notes would otherwise depend on the failed system, propose an authorized separate readable copy.

## Prepare questions and rehearsal records honestly

Build a small question sheet from the talk's actual claims, audience purpose and known constraints. Separate supported answers, facts requiring confirmation and decisions outside the speaker's authority. Do not invent hostile audience motives or confident answers about a roadmap, contract, price or result that the source does not establish. An acceptable holding line states the specific uncertainty without making an unapproved follow-up promise.

If rehearsal is requested, provide an observation sheet tied to segment anchors: actual elapsed time, unclear transition, missing dependency, display issue and proposed change. Leave it blank unless real observations are supplied or an authorized rehearsal is actually performed. A paper read-through is not audience feedback, a timing test or a live technology check.

When observations arrive, revise the affected cue or cut route and retain the previous revision and measured context. A shortened paragraph can reduce estimated speaking time; it does not establish a measured improvement until performed. Do not assess a person's accent, identity or fitness for a role; focus on understandable content and the user's chosen delivery goals.

## Acceptance, preservation and delivery

Return the usable cue sheet, full/short timing routes when needed, technology/fallback handoff and concise unresolved questions. A short talk may need only one compact document. Provide native speaker notes only when requested or clearly appropriate to the authorized file/audience; preserve the existing deck and useful notes when creating a separate candidate.

Check every cue against its exact anchor, important numbers/claims against the content, and every cut against its remaining dependencies. Recompute timing totals including questions and handoffs. Confirm that private notes and audience material are separated, fallback losses are explicit and estimated, reported and observed checks remain distinguishable.

For saved notes, reopen the actual destination and confirm anchor order, important wording, cuts and links. If the deck changed concurrently, reconcile affected anchors before replacing notes. Inspect uncertain saves before retrying. Do not claim the presentation is technically ready while a required audience-view or media check remains unperformed.

Stop at the requested preparation or revision. Sending material, recording, presenting live, changing access and contacting organizers remain separately scoped actions. A finished cue sheet establishes prepared delivery materials, not a successful presentation.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional speaker supplies a six-slide talk and a ten-minute slot including two minutes for questions. The proposed eight-minute speaking route allocates 1 minute to the opening, 2 to the problem, 3 to evidence, 1 to limits and 1 to the closing. Those estimates total eight minutes; no rehearsal has measured them.

The short route removes a ninety-second optional example from the evidence segment and replaces its later callback with a transition to the main finding. It retains the finding's limitation. The remote setup has only one known display, so a claim that notes will remain private is held until the actual share surface can be checked. A supplied PDF is a static fallback, with its absent animation disclosed.

No slides were changed, meeting joined, screen shared, rehearsal timed or audience response observed for this illustration.
