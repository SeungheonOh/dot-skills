# Plan role onboarding around useful work

Deliver a plan a newcomer and their host team can use from the first day. Connect each important responsibility to the knowledge, relationships, access and supported work that make it possible. A folder of reading links is not an onboarding plan, and a completed checklist does not establish competence or permission to operate independently.

Use [repository-onboarding-map](../repository-onboarding-map/SKILL.md) for understanding a codebase, [create-learning-materials](../create-learning-materials/SKILL.md) for authoring lessons, and [plan-project-delivery](../plan-project-delivery/SKILL.md) for delivering a defined project. This workflow coordinates a person's transition into an already established role. It does not determine employment eligibility, probation outcomes, compensation or access grants.

## Establish the role and planning boundary

Read the role's actual responsibilities, current team procedures, relevant existing onboarding material and the user's requested horizon. Identify:

- Whether this is a new team member, internal transfer or a changed responsibility within the same team
- Start date or relative start point, working windows, fixed commitments and any supplied ramp milestones
- The few role outcomes the person needs to handle, with current examples of acceptable work
- Relevant experience the person has chosen to share, without assuming competence or a deficit from a title
- Existing systems, access prerequisites, required organizational steps and their evidenced status
- Named host, buddy, subject experts or reviewers, including their agreed participation and available capacity
- Intended readers, requested format and destination, and whether this is a new plan or an update

Ask only for a gap that changes the useful plan. If dates are unknown, give dependency-ordered stages rather than inventing a start date. If the role itself is ambiguous, prepare common orientation and hold the role-specific work until its owner clarifies the expected responsibilities. A thirty-, sixty- or ninety-day structure is optional; use it only when it fits the request and actual work.

Keep the plan separate from private personnel records. Do not gather identity documents, health information, salary, personal circumstances or credentials to make an orientation packet complete. Record required administrative steps by name and supported contact route, without copying their sensitive contents. A requested plan does not authorize submitting employment forms, enrolling anyone, creating accounts, granting access or sending introductions.

## Map responsibilities to readiness dependencies

Choose representative tasks from the role's actual work. For each consequential task, establish what a useful result looks like and what must be available before an attempt. Make the distinction between these dependencies visible:

- **Context:** vocabulary, purpose, customer or stakeholder needs, prior decisions and boundaries
- **Procedure:** the applicable instructions, exact template, current example and quality checks
- **People:** who can answer a specific question, provide feedback or authorize a consequential action
- **Tools and access:** the minimum resource needed, request route, access owner and confirmation status
- **Practice conditions:** permitted sample data or environment, review support and effects of a real attempt

Use source versions or dated references when outdated material could change the task. A previous employee's plan is a starting point, not evidence that current tools, authority or contacts are the same. Mark obsolete, inaccessible or conflicting instructions with the exact question that needs resolution. Do not quietly replace a required procedure with a plausible tutorial.

Tie reading to a task or question. Instead of assigning an entire wiki, identify the short sections needed to explain a decision, complete a sample or locate the next step. Keep optional background distinct from prerequisites. Reuse a current accepted guide rather than recreating a manual in the plan.

Access states should distinguish needed, requested, confirmed for the required action and blocked. The host opening a link does not prove the newcomer can open it. A read permission is not an edit permission, and access does not itself establish decision authority. Record unknowns without testing another person's account or asking for passwords. No credential-sharing workaround belongs in the plan.

## Build a supported path to a first contribution

Select a bounded first contribution that is useful, reversible where possible, and reviewable with the available support. Define its input, expected output, quality checks, reviewer and action boundary. An internal draft may be suitable while customer-facing delivery remains gated. Do not manufacture busywork merely because it is easy to count.

Choose the support needed for each task rather than forcing a universal sequence. A person may read an example, observe a colleague, try a safe sample, perform work with a reviewer, then take an authorized independent task. Explain what each step establishes: observing shows exposure; a reviewed sample shows the result of that attempt; neither automatically grants production, financial or customer authority.

Keep the actual work and learning evidence concrete. “Prepare a draft weekly update using the current template; the host checks dates, sources and audience boundaries” is more usable than “learn reporting.” For a sample exercise, use approved fictional or minimized data and name its limits. If the only available task has live consequences, hold it for the proper approval or choose another authorized learning activity.

Attach feedback to the work: when it can be obtained, what the reviewer checks, how a correction is captured and which next step depends on it. Record the person's questions as questions, not as performance defects. Keep completion of training, successful task output and authorization for future work as separate states.

## Fit the newcomer and host workload together

Sequence prerequisites before dependent work and include host preparation that must happen before the newcomer can start. Show confirmed commitments separately from proposed help. Naming a buddy in a document does not obtain that person's agreement or create spare capacity.

Use declared working windows and known review availability. Account for ordinary meetings, setup and waiting time when they materially affect the plan. Do not treat an empty calendar as permission to fill every hour or compress required review to fit a target. When durations are uncertain, use provisional estimates and a checkpoint for revising them from actual progress.

Avoid scheduling several tasks that all require the same unavailable mentor. Offer supported independent work during an access or review delay, without disguising blocked practical work as completed reading. If a first contribution cannot fit the requested horizon, expose the dependency or capacity conflict and the smallest scope, date or support decision that would resolve it.

Make the first usable day clear: where to start, which materials are actually accessible, the first bounded task, the known help route and what remains pending. Keep social introductions purposeful and proposed unless arranged; do not require personal disclosures or invent meetings with colleagues.

## Deliver the two-sided onboarding packet

Return the actual editable plan. A short packet can combine:

1. **Start here:** role purpose, first-day route, help contacts with their scope, and immediate blockers
2. **Ramp plan:** task/outcome, prerequisite, resource, supported activity, expected artifact or check, timing, host/reviewer and status
3. **Host preparation:** access or material requests, accepted/proposed support, readiness checks and fallback work
4. **Checkpoints:** what the newcomer and host should inspect together, unresolved questions and the condition for changing the next stage

Include only fields that help this person act. An ordinary small-team transition does not need a separate dashboard or a numerical readiness score. Separate the newcomer-facing packet from any legitimately restricted host notes; do not hide a personnel assessment inside a practical learning checklist.

For an update, preserve completed work and actual feedback. Replan only the affected remaining tasks when access, support, role scope or pace changes. A missed session remains missed, and a delayed dependency stays visible. Do not backfill completion because its planned date passed or silently escalate the expectations attached to an already completed task.

## Acceptance, preservation and recovery

Read the packet as both newcomer and host. Check that:

- Every important in-scope responsibility has a supported first task or an explicit deferred/blocked disposition
- A newcomer can locate the next action and its input without relying on unexplained acronyms or an unintroduced contact
- Practical tasks distinguish observation, sample work, supervised work and authorized independent action where that distinction matters
- Access, policy and reviewer gates remain explicit; no calendar event or checklist creates authority
- The proposed load respects both newcomer and host constraints, with a useful route through a delayed prerequisite
- Claimed completed work has evidence for the actual outcome; unattempted work has no invented result

Save to the requested authorized destination and read back the saved content, links and status labels. Preserve source procedures and earlier progress records. If the destination changed concurrently, reconcile before writing; inspect an uncertain save before retrying. If placement is blocked, return the completed packet and the precise missing access or decision.

Stop when the requested plan or progress revision is usable and its important gaps are explicit. A plan is not an account setup, a booked meeting, a conducted training program, a measured productivity gain or an employment judgment. Separately requested external actions still need their actual recipient, data and action authority.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

An invented operations coordinator joins a team on November 9. The role includes preparing an internal weekly supplier update. The current template and a sanitized prior update are available. Casey has agreed to review one draft on Wednesday; access to the live supplier tracker is requested but unconfirmed.

The plan first uses the sanitized records to draft the update and asks Casey to check source dates, unresolved items and audience scope. This is a sample contribution, not a live status report. The live-tracker task remains blocked, with the existing access-owner route shown. If access arrives late, retain any actually completed sample and its review evidence while moving the real-data task; leave unfinished sample work pending, and do not mark the real-data task done from the sample alone.

These facts are invented. No person was assessed, account accessed, permission granted, meeting scheduled or contribution delivered by this illustration.
