# Reconcile job applications and next actions

Build or update the candidate's private tracker so they can see which roles they actually applied for, what was submitted, what the employer last communicated and what action remains. Preserve separate applications and incomplete evidence rather than forcing everything into a reassuring funnel.

Use [role-targeted resumes](../prepare-role-targeted-resume/SKILL.md) to create application material and [interview preparation](../prepare-job-interview/SKILL.md) for a specific upcoming stage. This workflow organizes the candidate's own process. It does not evaluate other applicants, infer protected traits, rank employability, negotiate terms or contact employers.

## Define the candidate and record boundary

Identify the user's own search, requested period or selected employers/roles, existing tracker, evidence cutoff and private output destination. Use authorized application receipts, sent messages, interview invitations, task notices, candidate-portal records and the user's own notes. A few supplied records are enough for a useful bounded tracker; do not require access to the entire mailbox or every job platform.

Read relevant available sources without changing them. Follow exact verified employer or portal references; a recruiting email's link is not automatically a trustworthy destination. Do not open shortened, token-bearing or suspicious links merely to populate a cell. Sign-in, new accounts, identity verification and disclosure through external forms retain their own authority and secure handling requirements.

Keep unnecessary sensitive data out: government identifiers, birth dates, health/accommodation details, demographic answers, background-check records, passwords and authentication links. The tracker normally needs an application reference and safe source locator, not the full application form. Compensation or offer details can remain in a separately restricted record when relevant; do not copy them into a broadly shared status sheet.

If the user wants an existing cloud tracker updated, confirm its actual audience before placing personal job-search material there. A familiar document name does not establish privacy. Preparing a private local artifact does not authorize an employer upload or sharing it with a current workplace, recruiter or coach.

## Establish vacancy and application identity

Keep the employer, exact role title, requisition/posting ID, location/working arrangement where stated, posting URL and source date. A title is not a unique key: two employers can use the same title, and one employer can reopen a role under a new requisition. Retain old posting facts when a page later changes rather than rewriting the history of the role applied for.

Separate the vacancy from each application attempt. Use the provider's application ID when available, together with employer/account context; otherwise create a private stable local ID and retain the supporting evidence. Distinguish saved interest, draft, referral, submission attempted and submission acknowledged. A downloaded resume or an interview-preparation note does not prove an application was sent.

Reconcile duplicate evidence by exact references and context. Multiple receipt emails may describe one submission; a second application to a reopened requisition may be a separate attempt. A recruiter forwarding a profile is not automatically a candidate's direct application. When correspondence cannot be matched reliably, keep an unmatched row with candidate links rather than attaching it to the nearest title.

For supplied counts, state whether they count vacancies, applications, submissions or events. Do not count every stage update as another application. Keep withdrawn or unsuccessful applications in history instead of deleting them to make the active list cleaner.

## Preserve the actual submission and event history

For each attempt, link the exact resume, cover letter, portfolio or requested answer version when supported. Record what evidence establishes it: sent attachment, confirmed upload record, retained submission copy or user report. A current profile document is not proof of what an earlier employer received. When exact version evidence is absent, say unknown; do not silently attach the most recently edited resume.

Create an event history only as detailed as the tracker needs. Each consequential event should retain its source, event date/time, observation date, exact application match and meaning. Keep automated acknowledgment, employer message, portal display and user-reported action distinguishable. A later observation may describe an earlier event; it is not necessarily a new stage.

Preserve raw employer wording beside any normalized stage. Use broad comparable labels only when they do not overstate the source, such as acknowledged, employer review reported, interview invited, interview scheduled, assessment requested, decision communicated or unresolved. Keep candidate task state separate from employer stage: an invited interview can still be unscheduled, and an assessment deadline can coexist with an old portal status.

Resolve apparent conflicts by checking application identity, event semantics and applicable source timing, not by always choosing the newest timestamp. A generic portal label and a specific employer email may have different coverage. Show both when material. The [MyGreenhouse candidate FAQ](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/43418495049499-MyGreenhouse-FAQ-for-Candidates) explains that employers choose which updates to share and that not every application appears. This illustrates why portal absence or an unchanged display cannot establish rejection or no activity; verify the actual provider's current meaning rather than applying one platform's labels universally.

Do not infer a rejection, withdrawal or closed process from silence, a vanished posting or a passed expected response date. Record explicit decisions at their stated scope. A vacancy being closed is not necessarily an individual outcome. An interview being scheduled is not evidence it took place, and a task sent to the candidate is not evidence it was completed.

## Turn records into a usable next-action view

For each active or unresolved attempt, identify the next supported candidate action, dependency, due date or trigger, and evidence that would complete it. Examples include choosing among offered interview slots, providing a requested ordinary document, completing an assessment under its actual rules, or reviewing a received decision. Keep completed actions separate from future proposals.

Preserve exact date/time, timezone and deadline semantics. Distinguish a fixed submission cutoff, an employer's estimated response window and a user-selected follow-up date. A date-only deadline stays date-only until its clock boundary is known. Do not invent end-of-day in the user's timezone for an employer's ambiguous cutoff. Flag the ambiguity and continue independent rows.

When a message requests scheduling, show the offered options and current response state; do not treat an unaccepted option as booked. When reschedule/cancellation evidence appears, retain the original event and link the change so the user sees the current supported arrangement without duplicate appointments.

For assessments, record the due date, instructions locator, allowed-aid constraints and completion/submission evidence. This tracker does not perform the assessment or help misrepresent unaided work. Keep access or login links private and avoid copying one-time links into shared artifacts.

A follow-up can be proposed when the user wants next-step planning, but its date and wording must not become an employer commitment or a sent message. Use an employer's stated timing where supplied; otherwise label a chosen check-in date as the user's planning choice. Do not create a universal rule that lack of a reply after a set number of days means rejection. Ongoing monitoring and reminders need their own requested scope.

Prioritize the view by evidenced deadlines, unresolved decisions and the user's explicit preferences. Do not invent numerical hiring probabilities or treat application volume, response speed or automated labels as measures of the person's worth or competence. Offers, withdrawals, acceptance and negotiation remain the user's decisions; merely recording a received offer does not accept it.

## Deliver and maintain the tracker

Return a usable private artifact, sized to the request. A compact tracker can hold:

- Stable application ID, employer, exact vacancy and application-attempt identity
- Submission state/date and exact submitted-material references or version gaps
- Last supported employer update, raw wording, source and as-of date
- Candidate next action, task state, deadline/zone, dependency and completion evidence
- Interview/assessment references, explicit final decisions and unresolved matching or status conflicts

Keep a short event/history sheet or linked notes only where needed to preserve meaningful changes. Separate the active next-action view from historical rows without deleting the evidence. Summaries must disclose scope, missing sources and unmatched records; an incomplete mailbox search is not a complete application inventory.

Check the saved tracker against the source set: each receipt or consequential event is assigned once or held unmatched, distinct attempts remain distinct, and current rows agree with their retained history. Verify that submission versions, deadlines and terminal outcomes have not been inferred from filenames, silence or an optimistic label. Read back requested cloud edits and confirm that unrelated user notes, formulas, manual priorities and audience settings remain intact.

When new evidence arrives, update the same stable application record and append material changes. If a prior match was wrong, preserve the correction and reassign only supported events; do not lose history by merging the rows wholesale. Before an uncertain save or update retry, inspect the target. Reconcile concurrent tracker edits rather than overwriting newer notes. If the source cannot be read, mark the affected status last-known and keep the actual observation date.

Stop when the requested tracker or update is delivered with clear next actions and coverage limits. Do not submit, reapply, withdraw, accept terms, send messages, book interviews, upload files, change privacy settings or create ongoing watches from a reconciliation request alone.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional candidate has two “Data Coordinator” records at Alder Museum: requisition A17 submitted in March and A29 submitted in June. A June receipt names A29, while an older rejection names A17. The portal shows an unchanged high-level label. A later employer email invites the candidate for A29 and offers two times; no reply is present in the supplied records.

The tracker retains the A17 decision, links the new invitation to A29 and lists choosing a time as pending. It does not merge the roles, mark an interview booked or infer rejection from the portal. The exact June resume version is unknown because only today's master resume was supplied, so the submission-material field remains unresolved.

No mailbox or portal was accessed, application submitted, interview booked, message sent or outcome predicted for this invented illustration.
