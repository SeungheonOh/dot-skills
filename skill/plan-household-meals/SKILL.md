# Plan meals that can actually be prepared and used

Deliver a usable meal calendar, a short preparation sequence and a clear account of which batches feed which meals. Choose dishes around the user's preferences and actual cooking capacity, then make quantities and carry-over explicit. Seven recipe links alone do not explain what to cook on a busy night or where Thursday's portions come from.

Use [grocery-list-reconciliation](../grocery-list-reconciliation/SKILL.md) once recipes and total yields are selected to scale ingredients, deduct confirmed stock and prepare purchase quantities. This workflow decides the menu and preparation sequence. It does not prescribe calories, weight change, medical diets or individualized nutrient targets; order food; or guarantee allergen safety or physical food condition.

## Gather the constraints that change the menu

Use the requested dates and meals, number of portions for each occasion, food preferences and explicit exclusions, meals already covered, existing recipes and usable stock information. Ask only about consequential gaps. Do not assume every day needs three planned meals or that every person eats an identical portion.

Establish the practical boundaries:

- Which meals need fresh preparation, packed transport, reheating or no cooking
- Available active cooking windows, acceptable elapsed time, and willingness to batch or repeat meals
- Actual equipment and relevant capacity, including oven/pan limits, refrigerator/freezer space and containers when carry-over matters
- Ingredients the user wants used, their reported quantity/form and known purchase, opening or preparation dates
- Budget if requested, currency, shopping opportunities and whether price evidence is available
- Requested output and whether recipe selection, an already chosen menu, or both are in scope

Use stated dietary constraints without inferring a diagnosis, personal health goal, age or weight. A portion count usually suffices; do not collect children's identities or ages for ordinary logistics. Keep health-related reasons and other private household details out of shared lists or external recipe queries. Search using the minimum ordinary ingredients/preferences needed, not a detailed household profile.

Treat supplied pantry records as reports, not an inspection. “Available” does not prove safe storage, freshness or enough quantity. Unknown condition or handling history must not become a recommendation to use the item. Continue with other ingredients or an explicit replacement option while the affected use is held.

## Select complete meals from usable recipe evidence

Respect dishes already chosen. When selection is requested, choose a manageable set from the user's supplied recipes or relevant current primary recipe sources, opening the actual instructions. Record recipe identity/link, yield basis, ingredients, active/elapsed times, equipment and any make-ahead or scaling limits needed for the plan. An inaccessible link or search snippet is not enough to establish its recipe.

Describe ordinary fit: preferred flavors, variety, convenience, ingredient overlap and the stated budget. Do not call a menu nutritionally complete, disease-appropriate or weight-loss effective. Preserve explicit exclusions and check the actual ingredients; do not silently substitute a similar dish. A missing compound-ingredient label or cross-contact information prevents an allergen-safety claim. If a stated allergy cannot be reliably accommodated from the available information, flag the affected choice for verification or select an alternative within the user's constraints without claiming it is guaranteed safe.

Match recipe yield to the required serving unit. “Makes twelve pieces,” “serves four” and a cooked mass are different bases. Use the user's portion choices or the recipe's stated serving basis, keeping uncertain appetites or piece-to-meal conversions visible. Do not turn a meal serving into a calorie prescription.

Include sides, accompaniments or sauces only when they are part of the requested meal or a clearly labeled proposal. Account for their preparation and ingredients. A dish with a ten-minute sauce and a forty-minute side is not a ten-minute dinner. Keep optional alternatives separate so a choice does not double the ingredient demand.

## Place meals and preparation in feasible windows

Build the menu around fixed meals away, busy nights, available shopping/preparation windows and the user's repeat-meal preferences. Put more involved cooking where there is real capacity, and use supported simpler meals or existing portions where time is limited. Do not assume an unrequested weekend cooking session, new appliance or extra shopping trip.

Separate active work, unattended cooking, cooling/storage tasks and total elapsed time. Preserve recipe timing assumptions and label user estimates. Two dishes can overlap only if equipment, temperature, pan space and the cook's active tasks permit it. A doubled yield does not automatically fit the same pan or take the same time. Follow source-supported batch sizes or show separate batches and their actual effect on the schedule.

Give each preparation block a concrete output: for example, the stated number of portions of one recipe, or an ingredient prepared for a specified later dish. Include prerequisites such as thawing when applicable, and do not invent a thaw duration or use counter thawing to meet a deadline. Keep cleaning and separation of raw and ready-to-eat foods in the sequence when their omission would create an unsafe shortcut.

If the menu cannot fit, explain the exact pressure point and offer a supported choice: another recipe, fewer optional sides, a smaller batch or a different preparation time. Do not shorten cooking, cooling or handling requirements to make the schedule look feasible. A provisional recipe time is a planning estimate, not a promise of dinner at an exact minute.

## Give every leftover portion one destination

Keep a compact batch record when meals depend on carry-over. Record recipe/batch identity, planned preparation date, total yield, portions eaten immediately, portions allocated to later meals, frozen portions and any unallocated remainder. Reserve the same portion only once. Leftovers are part of the original batch yield, not a second recipe to buy again.

For each later use, retain the source batch and storage/thaw/reheat requirements, including any original ingredient limitations. Reusing a component in a new dish must not silently reset its storage history. Keep raw, cooked, opened and frozen states distinct. An intended freeze is a future action, not evidence that the food was frozen or that it can now be used later.

Use applicable official food-safety guidance for the location, exact food and handling state, together with relevant package instructions. The U.S. [cold-food storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts) gives food-specific refrigerated limits and distinguishes them from freezer quality periods. Do not apply one leftover interval to every raw, opened or cooked item. Record the rule and its source beside any plan that relies on a storage interval; retain a shorter applicable limit rather than extending it for convenience.

The U.S. [four food-safety steps](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) support prompt refrigeration in shallow containers, keeping cold food cold, separating raw foods and safe thawing. For U.S.-based plans, its perishable-food limit is two hours outside refrigeration, reduced to one hour above 90°F/32°C. Include relevant handling steps rather than leaving a batch on the counter until a later prep block. These limits do not establish that a particular item's previous handling was safe.

Use the applicable [minimum-temperature guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) and recipe/package method for cooking or reheating. Do not substitute appearance, smell, tasting, a countdown timer or the plan itself for the required observation. If food-safety guidance is unavailable or conflicts with the proposed use, hold that carry-over and finish independent meals. Do not repair an unsafe or unknown history by advising reheating or freezing it.

Make storage labels useful without certifying the food: batch/dish, actual preparation date to be filled in, intended destination and applicable handling note. Planned dates should not be printed as if preparation already occurred. Include transport and destination refrigeration/reheating only when those capabilities are known; a packed lunch cannot assume an office refrigerator or microwave.

## Produce the handoff to shopping and day-of use

Deliver an easy-to-scan meal calendar with portions, recipe references, fresh/carry-over status, active/elapsed estimates and the important prep prerequisite. Pair it with the actual ordered prep list and batch allocations. A small plan can combine everything on one page.

For the grocery handoff, give selected recipes, source yields, total requested yields, chosen alternatives, planned batch arrangement and any confirmed stock reservations. Let ingredient arithmetic happen once in the grocery workflow. If a recipe changes, recalculate shared ingredient demand from source rows; do not merely add the new dish on top of the old one.

If budget is part of the request, separate known ingredient usage from actual package purchase costs. Use current relevant prices when claiming a priced plan, including the package and store basis. With no price evidence, report budget unverified or use an explicitly supplied estimate; do not claim the plan is under budget from recipe portions alone. Recipe selection does not authorize cart changes or purchases.

Offer a small fallback only when useful and within the user's preferences, using a known available meal or an explicitly proposed alternative. A fallback consumes its own stock and time. It is not permission to order delivery or buy replacements.

## Check, revise and preserve

Before delivery, verify that every requested meal is covered, intentionally excluded or visibly unresolved; every serving allocation has a valid source batch; and no batch or pantry reservation is used twice. Check cooking windows, batch/equipment constraints, storage intervals and dependencies in order. Keep missing quantities, labels, dates and handling history visible instead of treating them as zero or safe.

State what was actually checked: recipe sources, scheduling/portion arithmetic, storage guidance, prices, or saved-file content. Source review does not inspect the user's kitchen or prove safe food. Preserve original recipes, pantry records and prior plans. Save only to the requested authorized destination, read back the result, and inspect an uncertain save before retrying.

When a meal is skipped or delayed, do not simply slide every later meal forward. Recheck affected storage limits, actual preparation/freezing history, reserved portions and upcoming time windows. Keep consumed and prepared quantities separate from the original plan. Offer a safe alternative or hold an uncertain item; do not assume unused food remains suitable. Stop when the usable plan and specific remaining choices are delivered. No cooking, safety inspection, ordering, sharing or ongoing tracking has occurred merely because the plan exists.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional household wants two portions at dinner on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. A supplied stew recipe yields four portions and fits Monday's stated cooking window. Two portions go to Monday dinner and two to Tuesday dinner; they are one four-portion batch. Wednesday uses a separate two-portion recipe. The plan includes prompt cooling/refrigeration instructions and checks the applicable stew storage guidance before proposing Tuesday's reuse.

If Tuesday dinner changes, those two portions remain allocated but unconsumed. They do not become another four-portion batch, and the plan cannot assume they were frozen. A request to use them the following Monday requires checking actual handling and applicable limits, or replacing that meal; moving the label alone is insufficient. The grocery handoff requests four total stew portions and two Wednesday portions, not six stew portions.

The household, recipes, quantities and schedule are invented. This illustration was not cooked or executed and establishes no actual food condition, shopping cost, nutritional suitability or saved list.
