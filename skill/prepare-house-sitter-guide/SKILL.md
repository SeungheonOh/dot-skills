# Make the home usable without handing over the owner's accounts

Produce a concise guide a named sitter can follow in the actual home: how to perform the agreed routines, which controls to use, what to leave alone and who to contact when something differs. Keep a separate owner list for unconfirmed setup. A manual collection or a statement that “everything is smart” does not explain what the sitter can actually do.

Use [manual-to-task-checklist](../manual-to-task-checklist/SKILL.md) for one model-specific procedure and [home-maintenance-record](../home-maintenance-record/SKILL.md) for service history. This guide connects ordinary routines across devices and people for a temporary absence. It does not grant access, operate equipment or diagnose faults.

## Establish the stay, reader and real responsibilities

Use the user's agreed dates, sitter identity or role, accepted duties, approved contact route, relevant household notes, and device/manual details. Include times or timezone only where a timed routine or access window needs them. An intended return date does not prove the sitter accepted coverage or that someone will be available then.

Ask only about gaps that change the guide, such as whether the sitter is staying overnight, whether a task is agreed, or which of two identical-looking controllers operates the intended room. Do not request a full home inventory. Leave an unaccepted responsibility marked proposed rather than assigning it through polished prose.

Identify the intended private and sitter-facing destinations before copying household information. Vacancy dates, address, interior photos, contact details and security arrangements can be sensitive in combination. Use only details needed by the authorized reader; do not create a publicly accessible guide or QR link by default. Do not use unrelated calendars, personal messages or camera footage to reconstruct the household's routines.

Keep this workflow to ordinary non-hazardous household use. Childcare, medical care, pet medication or treatment, emergency-response planning, security-system procedures and electrical/gas/structural repairs need their own appropriate instructions and authority. Preserve the existence of a separate owner-provided plan without rewriting it as advice or copying sensitive details into this guide.

## Map routines to the control the sitter will actually have

Start with accepted activities, such as collecting deliveries, using room lighting, operating entertainment equipment or following a supplied plant-watering instruction. For each activity, identify:

- The room/object and recognizable control, using a neutral label or approved cropped photo when useful
- The agreed action or trigger, and the source of any schedule, quantity or setting
- Necessary device/app/account, connectivity or physical prerequisites
- The permitted operating range and any supplied “leave unchanged” instruction
- A recognizable normal result, a supported fallback, and the stop/contact condition

Write for the sitter's access, not the owner's. The owner being able to control a device remotely does not establish that the sitter can do so locally. A shared app, physical remote, voice assistant and wall switch can have different reach. Check which path is actually available; do not assume the sitter has the same phone, account, app version, language or accessibility preferences.

Group instructions by the real task rather than by manufacturer. For example, “watch the living-room television” may involve a display, input selection and separate volume control. Preserve their order and explain which remote controls which action. Do not invent menu labels, restart sequences or brand-specific behavior; use applicable supplied instructions or current official documentation for exact controls.

Label evidence proportionately: owner-reported, documented for the matched model, or actually observed in an authorized check. A manual confirms a supported procedure, not the current state of this home. If a control's identity or effect is uncertain, leave that path pending and give the supported alternative, if one exists.

## Separate access readiness from the everyday guide

Create an owner-only readiness list for prerequisites: the sitter's accepted duties, required existing access, an available physical control, relevant instruction review and the date/status of any real check. Keep proposed, requested, accepted and observed states distinct. The list should reveal what must be resolved before the sitter depends on it.

Never put passwords, Wi-Fi secrets, alarm PINs, lock codes, recovery codes, account sessions or credential-bearing links in either artifact. Record “owner to arrange access through the supported secure route” rather than adding a blank secret field. Do not suggest sharing the owner's account or weakening a safeguard. Sensitive access handoff and persistent permission changes require the applicable explicit approval or user handoff.

Do not assume a role called “guest” permits all everyday controls. For example, [Apple's Home sharing guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-mide/102386) distinguishes resident access from scheduled access to selected security-related accessories. It also notes that access through a separate accessory app can remain after removal from Home. Read the current documentation for the actual platform before describing a proposed access scope; this reference is not permission to invite anyone or change a lock.

Keep access setup and end-of-stay review in the owner's list, with the exact system and responsible owner when known. A scheduled expiry is a proposed or configured condition, not proof that every access route has ended. Do not revoke existing access, change permissions or clear accounts as automatic cleanup.

For cameras, microphones and recording devices, retain the owner's supplied disclosure and agreed boundaries if relevant. Do not activate, disable, relocate or inspect recordings to prepare the guide. If the sitter's expected privacy arrangements are unclear, identify that decision rather than inventing consent.

## Give usable fallbacks without inviting troubleshooting

For each important routine, describe what the sitter should do if the normal path is unavailable. Use only a supported low-risk alternative, such as an identified physical control, or a clear stop and contact route. Do not assume a smart device's physical control works without its hub or network; verify that dependency or leave it unresolved.

Avoid generic “reset the router,” “unplug everything” or “try the main switch” instructions. They can disrupt alarms, other residents, stored settings or an unrelated system. A guide to everyday use does not authorize resets, pairing, firmware updates, account changes, electrical work or repeated attempts at a lock.

Keep automated and manual routines distinguishable. If a supplied rule says an automatic action occurs at a particular time, explain whether the sitter should merely observe it or perform a separate agreed action. Do not schedule both as though they are independent duties. Unknown automation status is a question for the owner, not a reason to duplicate the physical action.

Give the contact route only with the user's authority for the relevant details and recipient. Preserve actual availability and decision limits; do not promise the absent owner will answer immediately or assign a neighbor responsibility without agreement. For a reported immediate hazard, stop ordinary operating guidance and use the relevant local emergency or qualified support route; do not improvise a household repair procedure.

## Deliver the two useful views

The sitter-facing guide should be short enough to use in the room. Lead with agreed scope, important limits and the contact route, followed by task-oriented instructions and supported fallbacks. Put detailed manufacturer references after the relevant task. A printable or offline-readable copy can help if the guide would otherwise require the same unavailable connection as the device.

The private owner list holds unresolved access/setup, acceptance of duties, evidence references and end-of-stay review. Exclude secrets from this list too. Add a small optional return note for actual issues, changes or unfinished duties. Leave future events blank; a return date passing does not complete the handback or prove access was removed.

Preserve source notes and previous guide versions. Reconcile changed dates, equipment or duties before replacing the existing guide; do not carry an old access check forward after a relevant configuration change. If a save is uncertain, inspect the exact destination before retrying. Reopen saved files and check that both views contain the right audience's information and the referenced controls still match the source.

## Acceptance and stopping condition

Read through an ordinary arrival, one routine device task, a lost-connectivity case and departure as document checks. Confirm that each accepted responsibility has a supported instruction or visible gap; each control is identifiable; the normal path and fallback respect the same limits; and no owner-only detail or secret entered the sitter copy. Distinguish this paper review from an actual device or sitter usability check.

Return the guide, private readiness list and consequential unresolved items. A completed document means preparation is complete, not that the home is accessible or every duty is covered. Sending the guide, inviting the sitter to an app, changing routines, testing devices or contacting others requires its own scope and approval. Honor a separately authorized delivery and verify the exact recipient and result before claiming it was sent.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional owner asks for a guide for a two-night stay. The sitter has agreed to bring in parcels and use the living-room lights. Owner-supplied notes identify a labeled physical light control and say an evening lighting automation already runs. App access has not been arranged, and the notes do not establish whether a second controller works without the hub.

The guide describes the labeled control from those notes and tells the sitter that the evening automation is expected, not a second manual duty. The second controller remains unresolved. The private list asks the owner to confirm the fallback and any needed access; neither view contains a Wi-Fi password, entry code or account invitation. Return notes are blank.

These are invented inputs illustrating the document choices. No device, account, property, sitter or permission was accessed, tested, changed or contacted.
