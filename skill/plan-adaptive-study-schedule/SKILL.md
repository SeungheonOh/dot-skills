# Plan an adaptive study schedule

Deliver an editable plan that says what to work on, when, what result to record and how to revise the remaining work when reality differs. Build around the learner's actual constraints and existing material. The schedule is a planning artifact; putting a topic on a calendar is not evidence that it was studied or learned.

Use [learning materials](../create-learning-materials/SKILL.md) to author lessons, [deliberate practice](../deliberate-practice-workbook/SKILL.md) to create focused drills, and [tutoring](../tutor-through-dialogue/SKILL.md) for an interactive explanation. This workflow allocates existing content and practice across time. It does not enroll the learner, book an exam, submit assessed work or make professional-certification claims.

## Establish the learning and time boundaries

Use the requested outcome, syllabus or resource list, actual assessment scope if supplied, and any existing plan. Identify the start date, deadline or review horizon, timezone when scheduling clock times, available study windows, fixed commitments and user-selected limits such as session length or days off. Ask only about missing facts that change the plan.

Distinguish hard deadlines from targets. Verify changing exam dates or official syllabus versions through the relevant official source when needed and authorized. Do not infer the exam scope from a similarly named course or promise that a third-party resource covers it completely. With no deadline, plan a useful bounded horizon and a stated review point rather than inventing urgency.

Use the learner's supplied attempts, completion records or stated familiarity as different kinds of evidence. Do not require a diagnostic exam for a clear scheduling request, infer ability from silence, or label the learner deficient because records are missing. Avoid collecting grades, health information or private calendar detail beyond what the task requires. An availability window is sufficient; the reason other hours are unavailable usually is not.

## Turn material into schedulable units

Map required topics or outcomes to exact existing resources, chapters, exercises or projects. Each useful unit needs a bounded activity, prerequisites, expected output or completion check, and an effort estimate with its basis. Keep navigation references precise enough to resume after an interruption. A link to an entire course is not an actionable forty-minute session.

Separate first exposure, worked-example study, independent practice, feedback/correction and later review when they consume different effort or provide different evidence. Do not equate watching a lesson with being able to solve a problem independently. Respect required course sequence, access dates, assignment rules and answer-reveal constraints.

Use observed pace when available, without extrapolating a single easy unit to every topic. Label instructor estimates and learner estimates separately. If pace is unknown, use provisional ranges and an early calibration session whose output is actual time plus unfinished work. Missing effort is not zero; mark affected feasibility uncertain. Do not invent exact durations merely to make the plan fit.

Identify dependencies that materially determine order. A practice unit requiring unavailable software, a prior concept or paid access remains blocked or conditional. Do not buy a resource, change the learning goal or substitute a different curriculum without authority. Continue scheduling independent units when possible.

## Fit work, review and recovery into real capacity

Calculate total schedulable study capacity from the learner's declared windows after fixed commitments, agreed breaks and setup time. Divide that capacity between planned learning work and recovery reserve; do not subtract the reserve twice. Avoid double-counting overlapping windows or treating all free calendar space as willingness to study. Preserve off days and explicit session limits. When dates or timezones are ambiguous, keep affected sessions provisional until resolved.

Place prerequisite work before dependent practice and leave room for feedback before a fixed assessment. Include review opportunities after initial study rather than allocating every available minute to new material. The [Institute of Education Sciences practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) recommends spacing learning and using quizzes to revisit content. It supports those principles, not a universal interval sequence, a personal learning-rate estimate or guaranteed exam success. Choose review timing from the available horizon, topic demands and observed performance; describe it as a planning choice.

Use existing appropriate exercises or retrieval prompts for progress checks. If new teaching content or a drill set is needed, identify that separate deliverable instead of disguising lesson authoring as scheduling. Keep permitted aids and success criteria explicit when comparison across attempts matters. A completed practice test is not automatically evidence of independent performance if answers were visible.

Reserve some capacity for unfinished work or correction according to the user's constraints and uncertainty; explain the chosen reserve rather than prescribing a universal percentage. Show estimated demand, planned allocations, unused reserve and work not yet placed in compatible units. A provisional plan whose low estimates fit but high estimates exceed capacity is conditionally feasible, not a promise.

If required work cannot fit, lead with the shortfall and realistic options: reduce optional coverage, extend the target if possible, use additional user-approved time, or acknowledge incomplete preparation. Do not remove required topics, sleep, breaks or existing commitments silently. Prioritize by supplied assessment relevance, dependencies and observed needs; missing topic weights do not authorize invented exam probabilities.

## Make the next session and revision rule explicit

Use a concise schedule with dates or ordered windows, unit/resource, activity, estimated duration, expected evidence and status. Exact clock times are optional when the user wants flexible daily blocks. Keep a small progress log for actual time, completed output, observed difficulty/error, remaining work and next review. Do not prefill performance or treat elapsed dates as completion.

Define how to revise from actual evidence:

- **Missed session:** retain it as missed/unattempted; move necessary work into available reserve or expose a revised shortfall. Do not stack it automatically on an already full day
- **Partly completed unit:** preserve the completed portion and source position; schedule only the remainder plus any needed correction
- **Longer-than-estimated work:** update comparable estimates cautiously, recompute remaining demand and show the impact on optional work and deadlines
- **Incorrect or aided performance:** retain the attempt conditions; schedule the appropriate correction or prerequisite review without claiming a general ability judgment
- **Demonstrated independent success:** reduce redundant work only within the supplied course/assessment requirements, and retain a later check when useful
- **Changed scope or deadline:** preserve the prior version and remap affected units rather than relabeling unstudied work as completed

Choose a review checkpoint tied to useful evidence, such as the end of the first study week or a completed practice set. The artifact can describe that checkpoint without creating a reminder, automation or ongoing commitment. Use new user-supplied progress to revise the schedule when requested.

## Acceptance and delivery

Deliver the requested dated schedule, a lightweight progress/review log and the important tradeoffs or uncertain inputs. A short plan can combine these in one document. Save to the authorized destination without altering the source syllabus or earlier progress records.

Read the saved result and check:

- Every required in-scope unit is scheduled, explicitly blocked, or visibly unplaced; optional exclusions are labeled
- Prerequisites, access dates, deadlines, off days and session limits are respected
- Allocated study and recovery time fit the declared windows without overlap or double counting
- Effort estimates retain their basis and uncertainty; work demand is not confused with elapsed calendar time
- Review and feedback have a place in the plan, and progress criteria distinguish activity completion from demonstrated performance
- A missed session can be handled by the stated rule without deleting evidence, inventing success or silently overloading later days

Report calculation, source, rendering and calendar checks separately. If exact time-zone conversion or rendering cannot be checked, name the affected limit. If a save outcome is uncertain, inspect the destination before retrying. Do not create calendar events, reminders, paid enrollments or external disclosures from a planning request alone. Stop when the plan and its revision method are delivered; neither an unattempted plan nor a fictional example establishes learning gains.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

An invented learner has four forty-five-minute windows next week and three existing spreadsheet lessons. Their provisional estimates are forty-five minutes for each lesson, plus thirty minutes of independent review: 165 minutes of work against 180 minutes of declared capacity, leaving fifteen minutes unallocated. The third lesson depends on the second.

The first week places the lessons in prerequisite order and reserves the fourth window for review and unfinished work. If lesson two takes seventy-five minutes instead of forty-five, forecast total effort rises by thirty minutes. The fifteen-minute reserve cannot absorb all of it. The revised plan must expose the additional fifteen-minute shortfall or propose a permitted scope/time change; it cannot mark the third lesson complete or assume a fifth window.

These are invented planning inputs and a paper illustration, not observed study behavior, an executed calculation tool, a saved calendar or evidence of mastery.
