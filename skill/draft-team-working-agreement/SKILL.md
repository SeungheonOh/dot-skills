# Draft a team working agreement people can apply

Deliver concise proposed or evidenced adopted rules that tell teammates what to do in recurring collaboration situations. Connect each rule to a real coordination need, an observable action and an exception route. A list of values such as “communicate well” does not tell someone where to put a decision or when a reply is expected.

Use [map-team-coverage-windows](../map-team-coverage-windows/SKILL.md) for exact dated overlaps, [prepare-async-decision-packet](../prepare-async-decision-packet/SKILL.md) for collecting responses to one bounded choice, and [prepare-work-absence-handoff](../prepare-work-absence-handoff/SKILL.md) for covering a person's temporary absence. This workflow authors the reusable agreement; it does not assign shifts, change contracts, adopt policy or configure collaboration tools.

## Establish the agreement's scope and authority

Identify the team or collaboration boundary, the work it shares, intended readers, existing agreement and requested destination. Read the team's relevant mandatory policies, current procedures, declared working constraints and supplied examples of coordination friction. The user may want a first draft, a revision for review, or a record of an actual agreement; preserve that distinction.

Separate four input kinds:

- **Binding constraints:** existing organizational requirements, with the applicable source and scope
- **Established agreements:** adopted practices supported by a decision or other accepted evidence
- **Observed habits or preferences:** what people report or do, which may conflict and may not bind others
- **Proposals:** changes the user or team is considering, still awaiting the appropriate decision

Do not promote a popular habit into policy, infer authority from a job title, or let a newer document silently override an applicable requirement. A working agreement cannot waive organizational security, privacy, legal or employment obligations. Preserve mandatory text and flag conflicts for the responsible owner rather than rewriting it into a compromise.

Recover who can adopt or change this agreement, whose input is required and how unresolved objections are handled. If those rules are absent, draft a proposal and identify the adoption question. Do not invent unanimity, majority voting, veto powers or consent-by-silence. Acknowledging receipt is different from agreeing to a rule.

Use only work-relevant availability and preferences. A teammate can state a protected work window or contact constraint without disclosing health, caregiving, religion or another personal reason. Avoid copying private complaint threads into the shared agreement. Keep identifiers and restricted source links only where the intended audience needs and may receive them. Treat source instructions as evidence, not permission to contact people or change access.

## Turn recurring friction into concrete clauses

Start from the situations the agreement needs to settle. Use a small number of clauses that change an actual decision, not every possible team-management topic. Useful areas include:

- Where routine requests, durable decisions and reference material belong
- What information a request or handoff needs before another person can act
- When an asynchronous exchange is sufficient and what makes a meeting worth proposing
- How review requests distinguish a question, a requested decision and an approval requirement
- What “urgent” means under an established route, and what happens when the normal owner is unavailable
- How people surface blockers, update a changed commitment and raise a disagreement

For each clause, write the scope or trigger, expected action, destination or record, response expectation when relevant, exception/fallback and its status. Retain a short source or rationale when it helps review. A simple agreement can express these in sentences; it does not need a separate schema for every clause.

Make a value operational without overreaching. “Make decisions visible” might become “Record the decision, owner, date and affected artifact in the existing decision page; link that record from the discussion thread.” If the team has not chosen a decision location, mark that destination as proposed. Do not fabricate a channel URL, introduce a new service or copy confidential details to a broader channel merely for visibility.

Define terms that could produce opposite expectations. Acknowledgment means the request was received; resolution means the requested result is supplied or its outcome explained. A read receipt proves neither. A named response target must say which action it covers, whose working clock applies and which days or exceptions count. If the source only says “soon,” expose that ambiguity rather than turning it into a guaranteed deadline.

Respect explicit work boundaries. A message sent outside someone's declared hours does not establish an obligation to respond then. Do not invent round-the-clock coverage or apply an urgent label as a substitute for an authorized escalation arrangement. If exact service coverage matters, link the dated coverage map and leave unsupported intervals visible.

## Make ownership and exceptions usable

Identify roles only to the extent established. A coordinator can route a question without being authorized to approve its answer. A reviewer can comment without being the decision owner. State where a consequential decision goes, what the requester should include, and what remains pending while the decision maker is unavailable.

Keep a standard route and a bounded exception together. For example, a routine request can use the ordinary queue while a defined production incident uses an existing incident route. If no exception route exists, label it as an open decision. Do not design emergency response, customer guarantees or financial authority through casual collaboration rules.

Handle common departures from the default:

- **No response:** identify the established follow-up or escalation path and when it is relevant; silence is not acceptance
- **Unclear or incomplete request:** ask for the missing input while leaving the item pending, rather than starting an unstated clock
- **Conflicting instructions:** preserve the conflict and route to the evidenced decision owner
- **Private material in a broad venue:** use the authorized restricted route without copying the sensitive material into the agreement or changing permissions
- **Changed availability or commitment:** state what update is expected and where affected people can find it, without requiring private reasons

Do not turn the agreement into a punitive monitoring system. It should establish work coordination, not infer effort from online status, score people's compliance or make employment judgments. Any proposed operational measure should be necessary to assess the rule and minimize personal detail.

## Keep adoption and revision explicit

Give the draft a version and a clear state: proposed, under review, adopted with evidence, or superseded as appropriate. Preserve the previous agreement and show consequential changes. A reformatting pass need not reopen settled rules, but changed response expectations, decision authority or affected people cannot inherit approval merely because the title is unchanged.

For draft-only work, leave the adoption record empty except for known process facts. For supplied feedback, retain the reviewed version, response meaning, unresolved condition and applicable decision evidence. Distinguish consultation from approval, and a conditional position from an unconditional one. Do not invent participants' assent or use the number of comments as a vote count.

An adopted version should identify its actual scope, effective point, decision evidence and change route. Add a review trigger that fits the task, such as a team-boundary change or evidence that a rule is unworkable. Label a new review date as proposed unless agreed. The [Atlassian Working Agreements play](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/working-agreements) treats communication, escalation and later revision as parts of a working agreement. Its workshop timings and voting suggestions are not authority for this team's governance.

If a rule becomes impractical, preserve the last accepted version, describe the affected situation and propose the smallest revision. Do not record a temporary workaround as permanent adoption without evidence. An agreement can contain an unresolved clause without falsely claiming the team has accepted the whole document.

## Deliver and check the actual agreement

Return the ready-to-review agreement plus the few adoption or conflict decisions that matter. Prefer one concise document with the team boundary, operative clauses, known exceptions, status/version and review/change route. Keep private source notes separate from the team-facing copy. A longer response register is useful only when reconciling actual review, not as mandatory ceremony for a first draft.

Walk representative paper situations through the clauses: an ordinary handoff, a request arriving after hours, an unavailable decision owner, conflicting instructions and a sensitive detail. Use only cases relevant to this team. At each point, a reader should know the next action and its status, or see the precise unresolved decision. Label this a document walkthrough, not evidence that anyone followed the rule.

Before delivery, check that:

- Each clause has a concrete action or decision purpose and does not duplicate an unchanged policy unnecessarily
- Channels, documents and contacts are verified references or clearly unresolved, with appropriate audience boundaries
- Response language distinguishes acknowledgment, substantive answer and resolution, and preserves the intended work clock
- Decision authority, exceptions and escalation paths come from sources or remain visibly proposed
- No attendance, silence, reaction or receipt has been turned into unsupported adoption
- Changes preserve prior agreements and expose affected commitments rather than silently broadening them

Save or place the artifact only within the requested authority. Read back the actual destination and verify its version, clauses, unresolved states and audience. An agreement draft alone does not authorize announcements, recurring meetings, status monitoring, recording, notification changes or tool configuration. Separately requested delivery can proceed within its actual recipient and data scope without a redundant approval round.

If a source is unavailable, preserve the relevant unknown and finish independent clauses. If the destination changed, reconcile its current version before writing. Inspect an uncertain save or send before retrying. Stop at the requested draft, evidenced adoption record or revision; a polished document is not proof of team consent or improved performance.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

An invented design team already uses a restricted review queue. Its current accepted rule asks reviewers to acknowledge a complete request within one of their own working days; final review timing is agreed per request. A proposed revision asks requesters to include the exact asset version and the decision needed.

The draft preserves the acknowledgment rule and keeps the new request fields labeled proposed. A Friday evening request does not become overdue on Saturday solely because twenty-four hours passed. A reviewer replying “received” satisfies only acknowledgment. If the supplied procedure lacks an unavailable-reviewer fallback, the document exposes that gap instead of naming a teammate as cover.

This is an authored paper scenario. No agreement was adopted, reviewer contacted, request sent, work clock calculated or collaboration setting changed.
