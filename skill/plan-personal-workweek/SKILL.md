# Make a workweek fit the work and the person

Deliver an editable week plan that identifies what the user can reasonably attempt, what cannot fit, and what decision or input would change that. Connect actual tasks to usable work windows and completion checks. A ranked to-do list does not establish capacity, and a calendar full of blocks does not establish that the work will finish.

Use [plan-project-delivery](../plan-project-delivery/SKILL.md) to decide how a project reaches acceptance, [issue-backlog-prioritization](../issue-backlog-prioritization/SKILL.md) for a rubric-based issue comparison, and [inbox-action-triage](../inbox-action-triage/SKILL.md) to discover obligations in messages. This workflow combines already identified responsibilities into one person's bounded week. It does not turn a new request into an accepted commitment or allocate another person's time.

## Establish the week and the real work boundary

Use the task list, current plans, relevant commitments and availability supplied or authorized for this request. Establish the week's dates, timezone, working windows, hard deadlines, known fixed commitments, breaks/off-time, and requested output. Respect a flexible daily plan when the user does not want exact clock times. A supplied availability list is sufficient; a calendar connection is optional.

Find the few choices that materially change the plan:

- Outcomes the user wants to protect this week, including commitments to others and the user's own priorities
- Current task state, remaining effort or range, estimate basis, prerequisites, and a useful completion condition
- Which work needs uninterrupted time, a particular setting/tool, an external response, or a specific working window
- Recurring operational load, setup/transition time and any allowance for incoming work
- Which dates and obligations are fixed, which are preferences, and which proposed changes require the user's decision

Do not infer willingness to work from empty calendar space. Use private calendar content only as far as necessary: blocked intervals usually suffice, without names, titles or personal reasons. Do not infer health, family circumstances or productivity from availability, online presence or missed tasks. Keep the personal plan private unless sharing is separately authorized.

When duration, priority or availability is missing, continue with supported work and make the affected allocation provisional. Use a clearly labeled planning estimate only when it has a defensible basis or the user requests one. Do not invent effort to fit the desired finish date. For genuinely competing commitments whose tradeoff is not established, present the choice rather than silently accepting a missed obligation.

## Reconcile tasks before allocating time

Identify each outcome once across lists, notes and project plans. Keep the source identity or useful link; similar titles alone do not prove two entries are the same work. Distinguish accepted commitments, incoming requests, personal goals, maintenance work and ideas. Check current evidence for completion or cancellation before carrying old tasks forward.

For each retained task, record only the fields needed to schedule it: short ID/title, next useful action or deliverable, completion check, remaining active effort, deadline type/date, readiness/dependencies, required session shape and priority basis. Distinguish active effort from waiting and elapsed turnaround. A review can require twenty minutes of the user's time yet depend on a two-day response window.

Break a large outcome into honest resumable units when the work allows it. Give a partial unit its own output, such as a marked-up section or reconciled subset, while retaining the unfinished parent outcome. Do not split work that actually requires a single uninterrupted session merely to make it appear to fit. Include necessary review and handoff effort; writing the draft may not complete the commitment to deliver an approved copy.

Keep blocked work important but unscheduled for execution until its prerequisite is available. An authorized preparation step may be ready independently. Do not assume a colleague will respond on an invented date, assign them work, or count the same prerequisite effort under every dependent task.

## Build capacity without double counting

Derive usable windows from the user's declared work time and existing fixed commitments. Reconcile overlapping source intervals before totaling them. Treat date-only deadlines as dates; if a same-day ordering decision depends on a precise cutoff, resolve it rather than inventing 17:00. Use actual date-specific timezone rules for clock-time plans, including travel or daylight-saving changes when relevant.

Separate these quantities in compatible units:

- Gross declared work capacity
- Fixed commitments already occupying that capacity
- Necessary breaks, transitions and routine work not already included elsewhere
- Remaining flexible capacity
- Planned task effort, reserved contingency and any still-unallocated time

Each allowance belongs in the accounting once. If daily availability already excludes meetings and lunch, do not subtract them again. If email handling is a scheduled task, do not also charge the same time as routine overhead. Keep reserve visible as uncommitted capacity; do not simultaneously allocate it to promised work. Explain its chosen size from the user's uncertainty or preference instead of imposing a universal percentage.

Check the shape as well as the total. Four fifteen-minute gaps do not necessarily provide one hour of concentrated work. Use the user's actual session constraints, equipment and location requirements. Do not claim multitasking creates extra capacity or count simultaneous meetings and focused work as both fully available.

Compare remaining effort with usable capacity, preserving ranges. If low estimates fit but high estimates do not, call the plan conditional and show which work is exposed. Unknown-duration work needs a bounded discovery step or an explicit unresolved allocation, not a zero-minute estimate. A planning allowance for discovery is a limit on effort, not a promise that discovery will succeed within it.

## Place work and expose the tradeoffs

Place genuinely fixed commitments first, then work with hard prerequisites or cutoffs, and then flexible priorities in compatible windows. Preserve the user's preferences for focus periods or batching where they fit. Do not present an arbitrary scheduling heuristic as an optimal solution.

For each proposed block or daily allocation, state the task, intended output, estimated effort and start condition. Distinguish an attempt budget from a completion forecast. Leave external waiting visible between stages rather than filling it with fictitious productive effort. Use a short overflow section for work that is blocked, deferred by the proposed plan, or not placeable under current constraints.

When the week is overcommitted, lead with the shortfall and its consequence. Offer concrete alternatives within the known facts: reduce optional scope, sequence a smaller useful increment, propose a different deadline, or use additional time only if the user chooses it. Do not remove breaks, work outside declared hours, downgrade a required outcome or change someone else's expectation to balance the arithmetic. A proposed postponement remains pending until the user accepts it; preparing the plan does not notify anyone.

Give the user a usable first action. For uncertain large work, an early inspectable slice can improve the estimate; record what it should reveal and when the remaining plan needs reconsideration. Avoid a week of vague labels such as “work on project” that cannot produce progress evidence.

## Define how the plan changes after reality arrives

Keep a lightweight update line for actual time, completed output, remaining effort and the next start point. Future status stays unattempted. A block ending, a date passing or a task being scheduled does not establish completion.

- **Interrupted or partial work:** preserve the output and restart point; update remaining effort from what is left, without charging the full original task again
- **Missed block:** keep it missed; move remaining work only into genuine spare capacity or expose a new conflict
- **New request:** distinguish request from commitment, identify the work it would displace, and ask for a material priority decision when necessary
- **Dependency arrives late:** recheck the remaining compatible windows and downstream cutoff; do not compress review or quietly create overtime
- **Estimate changes:** revise affected comparable work cautiously and recalculate remaining demand, leaving completed work intact
- **Higher-priority interruption:** retain the interrupted task and record the proposed displacement, including any commitment that now needs renegotiation

Include a review point appropriate to the request, such as after the first uncertain work unit or at the end of the week. This is a note in the artifact, not an automatic reminder or ongoing monitoring agreement. Do not carry every unfinished idea into next week by default; reconsider whether it still belongs in scope.

## Deliver, verify and stop at the plan

Return the actual daily/block plan, a concise capacity summary, visible overflow/blocked work and the revision rule. A small week can fit on one page. Preserve source task lists, existing calendar events and previous plan versions. When saving is requested, use the authorized destination and inspect the saved copy; reconcile concurrent edits and inspect uncertain saves before retrying.

Check that every in-scope commitment is placed, explicitly conditional, or visibly unresolved. Recompute allocations from the underlying windows, verify no task or allowance is counted twice, and check prerequisite order, deadline meaning and uninterrupted-session requirements. Confirm that the plan's completion language matches the available evidence and that its first action is actually ready.

Report source coverage and calculation checks separately from any unperformed calendar or application check. Deliver useful supported allocations even when one estimate or source is missing. Stop when the plan and its consequential decisions are clear. Creating events, reminders, tracker edits, delegation or messages about changed commitments requires its own scope and authority.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional user declares twelve hours of flexible work time after meetings and breaks. Two hours are already reserved for routine administration, leaving ten. They choose one hour of contingency. A report needs four hours including its review, a proposal needs three, and a new analysis request is estimated at two to four hours. The request has not been accepted.

The report and proposal use seven hours. Two hours remain for planned work after preserving the contingency. The analysis fits only its low estimate, so the plan cannot promise its completion. It offers a two-hour investigative slice with an explicit remaining-work check, or asks which existing allocation should change before accepting the full request. A later one-hour interruption consumes contingency once; it does not create another available hour.

These are invented planning inputs and arithmetic, not observed effort, a saved calendar, an accepted request or evidence that the work was completed.
