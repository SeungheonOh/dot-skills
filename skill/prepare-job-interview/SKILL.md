# Prepare for a job interview with evidence and usable practice

Deliver materials the candidate can actually rehearse and consult: a compact role brief, answer outlines grounded in their experience, a small bank of reusable examples, relevant practice prompts and informed questions for the interviewer. Advice to research the company or use a framework is not a substitute for preparing those materials.

Use [prepare-role-targeted-resume](../prepare-role-targeted-resume/SKILL.md) when the requested output is a resume. Use [deliberate-practice-workbook](../deliberate-practice-workbook/SKILL.md) for a separate sequence of focused drills, and [tutor-through-dialogue](../tutor-through-dialogue/SKILL.md) for sustained teaching. This workflow prepares the candidate for a particular role or interview stage; it does not assess other applicants or predict an employer's decision.

## Establish the actual interview and evidence boundary

Inspect the job description, invitation or stage instructions, submitted resume/application, and career examples the user supplies. Establish the role and employer, interview stage and format if known, available preparation time, requested materials and destination. Preserve a specified language, length and help style. A missing interview date need not block a useful packet.

Distinguish confirmed format from a plausible practice choice. A title such as engineer does not establish a coding exercise; a panel does not establish each person's topic. Record known assessment instructions, permitted aids and requested presentation or portfolio material. When format is unknown, prepare transferable core answers and label stage-specific prompts provisional rather than manufacturing the interview process.

Use current official employer material for claims about its work, product and stated requirements when research is in scope. Keep source dates and exact vacancy identity where they matter. A job posting establishes what the posting says; it does not reveal the interviewer's hidden rubric, motives or exact questions. Do not treat anonymous question lists as confirmed upcoming questions or use improperly obtained assessment material.

Do not require private personnel files or a full employment archive. The user's account can support a preparation example, labeled as self-reported where verification matters; absence of documents does not imply the experience is false. Ask about contradictions that would change an answer, such as individual responsibility or an outcome's attribution. Continue supported preparation while holding the disputed claim.

## Map the role to the candidate's real examples

Identify the few responsibilities or capabilities most relevant to this stage. Connect each to a concrete example, a useful question family, and any remaining gap. Distinguish direct experience, related transferable experience, knowledge without an applied example, and not yet evidenced. These are preparation states, not eligibility findings or a numerical fit score.

Select examples for complementary coverage rather than inventing one story per keyword. A coordination example may support prioritization and communication, but the answer must address the question actually asked. Include an alternative where reusing the same example would hide a missing capability. Volunteer work, education and personal projects keep their actual labels.

For each chosen example, create a short story card:

- The question types it can genuinely address
- Context and objective, with only details needed to understand the situation
- What the candidate personally did, their decision and relevant alternative or constraint
- Observed outcome, attribution and any limits; an unknown outcome stays unknown
- What they learned or would change, only if supported by the candidate's account
- A source or private fact-check note, plus likely clarifying follow-ups

Separate personal actions from team work. Do not promote participation to leadership, a team's result to an individual achievement, exposure to expertise, or a proposed change to completed work. Preserve approximate quantities and their period, units and basis. If there is no measured benefit, describe the supported result or scope instead of adding a persuasive percentage.

Situation, task, action and result can organize a behavioral example. The [National Careers Service STAR guidance](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method) describes this structure and encourages concise examples and follow-up preparation. It is an optional aid, not a rule that every answer needs four scripted sections or proof that an employer uses that rubric.

## Write answer outlines that remain the user's answers

Prepare the requested opening introduction and the most relevant responses. Use natural speaking notes or concise draft wording, preserving the user's voice. Do not invent enthusiasm, career goals, reasons for leaving, salary expectations, willingness to relocate or other commitments. If motivation is missing, provide a factual connection to the role and one specific question for the user rather than a fabricated first-person belief.

Make each answer do its own job:

- **Introduction:** connect the relevant present or recent work, a small amount of supporting history and the user's stated reason for this next step
- **Behavioral question:** choose an actual example, emphasize the candidate's action, and retain the outcome's limits
- **Experience gap:** acknowledge the exact missing experience, describe supported adjacent work and explain a plausible approach as a proposed approach
- **Technical or practical reasoning:** clarify the problem, expose assumptions, compare alternatives and explain how a result would be checked; hypothetical reasoning must not become past experience
- **Difficult history question:** use only facts and explanations the candidate chooses to share; do not invent an explanation, change chronology or imply that private personal circumstances must be disclosed

Prepare follow-ups that test the story's substance: what was their part, why that choice, what evidence supports the result, and what changed afterward? A polished headline that cannot survive those questions needs a narrower claim or a fact check.

Keep private coaching and source notes separate from material intended to be shown. Remove confidential customer names, private repositories, credentials and unnecessary colleague details from speakable examples. Generalize context without changing the achievement. If the claim cannot be explained without unauthorized disclosure, use a different example or flag that limit. Do not publish or upload recordings, source records or application material to a practice service merely to create the packet.

## Build useful practice and questions to ask

Provide a bounded practice set matched to the known stage and time available. Label questions as authored practice, employer-supplied instructions or publicly documented examples. Never present generated questions as a leaked or predicted interview script. For technical practice, distinguish original fictional exercises from any actual assessment and preserve its permitted-aid rules.

Give each practice prompt a purpose and observable feedback criteria, such as answering the actual question, identifying personal contribution, explaining a tradeoff and supporting a result. Keep learner-response and performance fields empty until there is an actual attempt. Requested sample answers should be labeled as source-grounded drafts, not recorded user responses. A text outline cannot establish speaking pace, delivery or performance under interview conditions.

If a mock conversation is requested, ask one question at a time and respond to the user's real attempt. Identify the most consequential improvement with evidence from that answer; allow a revision or a new question according to their preference. Do not perform both sides, infer competence from silence or give an unsupported hiring probability. A requested packet alone does not require an interactive mock session.

Write a few questions the candidate can use to learn about the actual role: success expectations, work boundaries, current challenges, collaboration and the next stage. Tie researched questions to an inspected source and an unresolved issue. Avoid pretending an interviewer holds a particular view. Do not add negotiation demands or acceptance commitments under the heading of questions.

Include a short day-of checklist only when useful: the confirmed format, requested materials, exact local date/time and contact route if supplied, and outstanding access or equipment checks. Keep an unknown link or unverified device check visible. Preparing this list does not test equipment, contact the employer or change an appointment.

## Deliver, check and recover

Return the actual preparation packet, sized to the request. A compact packet can combine the role priorities, introduction, story cards, practice prompts, questions to ask and important unresolved facts. Put the highest-value next practice first; do not turn a short preparation request into a full curriculum.

Before delivery, check that:

- Every personal claim matches a supplied account or source at its stated strength, including dates, numbers, ownership and completion status
- Each answer addresses its prompt, and follow-ups do not require invented detail
- Employer facts have applicable sources; authored questions remain explicitly practice
- Missing experience is handled honestly without becoming a negative hiring verdict
- Confidential material and private coaching stay out of anything intended for the interviewer
- Actual attempts, observed feedback and unattempted practice remain distinct

Preserve the original resume, notes and user attempts. Save a separate version to the authorized destination and read back the exact delivered copy. If the role or interview stage changes, revise affected priorities and prompts without silently overwriting earlier answers or feedback. Inspect an uncertain save before retrying. When an input is missing, deliver the supported material and name the smallest fact needed to finish the affected answer.

Stop at the requested packet or mock-practice exchange. Contacting an employer, booking practice with someone else, recording, submitting an assessment, sending a follow-up or accepting an offer requires its own authority. Preparation quality does not establish interview success or future competence.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

An invented applicant is preparing for an operations interview. Their notes say they maintained a delivery tracker and flagged two late shipments to a supervisor; the supervisor chose replacement suppliers. There is no measured cost saving.

The story card supports coordination and escalation. Its action section describes updating the tracker, checking dates and presenting the two exceptions. The result stays at “the supervisor had the exceptions for the ordering review.” It does not claim that the applicant led procurement or reduced delays. A follow-up asks how they decided which shipments to flag; that detail remains a question until the applicant supplies it.

The practice set contains an authored prioritization question, and the feedback field remains blank. No interview, recording, employer contact or hiring outcome occurred in this illustration.
