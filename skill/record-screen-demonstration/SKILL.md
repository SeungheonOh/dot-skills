# Record a demonstration someone can follow

Produce the actual screen recording requested, showing the intended interface and observable result clearly. A moving preview, recording indicator or successful file save is not enough: the delivered file must contain the right surface, necessary sound and complete action at a readable size.

Use [prepare-product-demo](../prepare-product-demo/SKILL.md) if the scenario or presenter cues still need substantial development, [create-edited-video](../create-edited-video/SKILL.md) for editorial assembly, and [caption-delivery-pass](../caption-delivery-pass/SKILL.md) for requested caption tracks. This workflow captures an established scenario; it does not authorize broadcasting, recording a meeting or publishing the result.

## Define what may be captured and done

Establish the intended audience/use, app/workflow, starting state, required visible result, approximate or hard duration, viewing size, output format/destination and any narration or application-audio requirement. Identify the available recorder, actual computer and display/window to use. State whose computer/browser will be recorded; do not imply access to the user's screen from an unrelated cloud environment.

Separate authority for recording from authority for actions shown. Recording a task does not authorize sending messages, buying items, creating accounts, changing permissions or editing real records. Use already-approved sample state and harmless actions when possible. A training workspace may still send email or trigger integrations. Keep any consequential step and reset action within its actual authorization; otherwise stop before it and describe that limitation truthfully.

Use the user's available supported capture tool when suitable. Inspect its version, permitted sources and save behavior before choosing settings. Installation, new screen/microphone permissions, access to another app and upload to a recording service need the applicable authority. Do not broaden capture permissions or switch to a more intrusive tool to evade a denial. If capture capability is unavailable, deliver the useful setup/cue material and exact blocker without claiming a video exists.

Keep other people's calls, private conversations and background microphone capture outside this workflow. Do not record a person without the required consent. A request for screen video alone does not imply webcam, microphone, system audio or all-monitor capture. Do not bypass protected-media capture restrictions or reuse unapproved music/content merely because it is playing on screen.

## Prepare a limited capture surface

Prefer the narrowest source that includes the complete task. Inspect what the chosen mode actually captures: a window source may omit a popup, while a display or region source may include another window placed over it. A selected app title is not proof that later dialogs or replacement windows remain within the same safe boundary.

Before capture, inspect the actual surfaces that may appear: tabs, address bars, notifications, account menus, recent files, autofill, search suggestions, file dialogs, paths and terminal prompts. Use fictional or expressly approved minimized data. Keep authentication and secret entry outside the recording; do not rely on a planned blur to protect a password or token during capture. A crop or overlay can fail after a resize or scene switch, so remove sensitive content from the capture path where possible.

Preserve existing work and recorder settings. Record the source/scene, output path, selected audio routes and any settings changed for this task. Create a separate supported recording profile/scene only when useful and authorized; do not overwrite a user's broadcast setup. Close or rearrange only approved windows, and do not change global notification, display or privacy settings as an assumed prerequisite.

Frame the app so necessary controls, labels and results are legible in the intended output dimensions. Check browser/app zoom, display scaling, capture crop and recorder canvas/output scaling together. A high-resolution desktop shrunk into a small video can make text unusable. Preserve enough context to understand the task and avoid stretching. Verify cursor visibility when pointing is part of the explanation; do not depend on cursor motion alone to communicate a critical value.

Confirm that the planned sequence does not wander outside the approved surface. For an unavoidable file picker or second window, plan a safe source transition and inspect it before the full take. If the tool cannot capture that step without exposing unrelated content, change to a supported authorized route or mark the missing step; do not silently expand to all displays.

## Bind audio and output to the actual recording

Select only requested audio sources. Keep microphone, application/system audio, monitoring output and recorded tracks distinct. A meter showing signal does not prove that the signal is on the track a normal viewer will hear. Avoid recording the same device through both a global and scene-specific route, which can duplicate sound; [OBS's audio-source guidance](https://obsproject.com/kb/audio-sources) describes this particular interaction.

For separate editorial tracks, also establish the intended playback mix or clearly identify that further editing is required. [OBS's multi-track guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/multiple-audio-track-recording-guide) notes that ordinary players typically play one track at a time. Do not put narration and essential application audio only on separate tracks and call a silent or incomplete default mix viewer-ready. Inspect all recorded tracks for unintended captured content, even if the default player does not expose them.

Choose an existing supported recording preset that meets the viewing requirement without unnecessary load. Establish a unique filename or take identity, writable destination, sufficient available space for the expected duration, and how recording is stopped/finalized. Keep recording and streaming controls distinct. Do not activate a stream, cloud upload, replay buffer or background recording merely because the application offers it.

Use a supported recording container and recovery route appropriate to the tool. For example, [OBS's output guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/standard-recording-output-guide) recommends MKV for resilience to an ungraceful stop and provides a remux route when MP4 is required. Follow the current tool's actual options; do not treat a file extension change as conversion or claim that a robust container guarantees every frame survived. Preserve the original capture when making a delivery derivative.

## Record and review a short pilot

Before a long take, capture a small representative segment within the authorized scope. Include a meaningful interface change, the smallest text the viewer must read, the planned popup/source transition if any, and a brief intentional audio sample when audio was requested. This is a recording and must obey the same privacy limits as the final take.

Stop and allow the tool to finalize the file. Open that saved file rather than judging only the live preview. Check:

- Correct surface and framing before, during and after the representative action
- Readable labels/results at the intended viewing size; no important control outside the crop
- Actual moving picture, acceptable motion for the task and no blank/frozen interval
- Intended audio present, intelligible and aligned with visible events; no duplication, clipping or unrelated sound
- No unexpected content in other audio tracks, source transitions or captured edges

If playback/listening is unavailable, say which checks could be performed and which remain unverified. Metadata or frame extraction alone does not establish that audio was heard or the entire motion reviewed. Do not commit to a long recording while a material capture failure remains unexplained.

Correct the smallest relevant setting or source within scope and make a fresh pilot when needed. A black window does not authorize disabling security or hardware protections. Use the official documentation for the current capture tool and OS; [OBS's source guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/sources-guide) illustrates why source types differ across platforms. If a safe supported source cannot include the necessary content, report that boundary.

## Capture the intended take with honest continuity

Return the application to its approved starting state without deleting or undoing unrelated work. Confirm source identity, recording destination, audio routing and private-content boundaries after any app/window restart. Start recording only when the task is ready, and verify the recorder's actual active state.

Perform the established route at a pace the viewer can follow. Allow important menus, entered values and results to remain visible. Use narration to explain the action when requested, without asserting success before the relevant result appears. A preexisting record is sample setup, not a result created during the take.

If the demonstration encounters an unexpected prompt, private information, consequential action boundary or uncertain write, stop the dependent action and pause/stop capture through the supported control. Verify that capture really stopped before authentication or private work. Do not assume a hidden window, mute icon in another app or paused preview stops the recorder.

After an uncertain side effect, establish its actual outcome before a retake; do not submit again just to obtain cleaner footage. If a reset is outside the approved scope, retain the partial take and ask for the exact decision needed. Use a new take identity for a restart. Keep a brief take/segment record when continuity matters: starting state, actions actually performed, interruptions and what was not captured.

A recording can be a complete continuous take or an explicitly identified set of segments. Do not present stitched segments as uninterrupted execution or remove a consequential failure to imply a successful live run. If editing is requested, preserve the source captures and carry the segment history to the video-editing workflow. Do not fabricate missing screen states, outcomes or narration as evidence that the application performed them.

## Verify the file the user will receive

Stop recording and confirm finalization. Identify the resulting saved take, size, duration and actual content; an earlier file with the same title is not this take. If stop/save is uncertain, inspect the recorder state and candidate files before starting another capture or closing the app. Do not force-quit a recorder that may still be finalizing merely because a file is visible.

Open the exact delivery file. Watch and, when relevant, listen through a short demonstration. For longer recordings, cover the whole requested sequence and state any unreviewed intervals; privacy-sensitive unreviewed portions cannot be treated as cleared for sharing. Inspect the opening, each action/result, transitions and ending. Check all included audio tracks and any requested remux/conversion independently of the original capture.

Acceptance requires that the saved file:

- Shows the requested start, meaningful actions and evidenced ending without an unexplained missing step
- Is readable at the intended viewing size and contains the necessary cursor/control context
- Contains only authorized visual/audio sources, with no unintended content in alternate tracks
- Has usable expected audio or is correctly described as silent; matches the requested delivery format
- Preserves honest segment continuity and distinguishes observed outcomes from explanation or prepared sample state

Technical decoding and duration checks support this review but do not replace it. A successful remux cannot repair a missed dialog or absent microphone. If the recording is incomplete, label the affected take/interval and retain useful segments; do not call it the finished demonstration.

If private or credential-bearing information was captured, hold the file from delivery and identify the affected interval privately without repeating the secret. Stop further capture, minimize additional copies and use an authorized local removal/re-record route. Verify the exact sanitized derivative across every stream before sharing; an overlay in the editor preview is insufficient. Do not upload the raw take to a new service for cleanup or delete the only recoverable source without the required authority.

## Finish without leaving capture running

Deliver the verified video or approved segments, with duration, recording context, meaningful continuity limits and the actual review coverage. Attach a compact take index only when needed for later editing. Sharing beyond the user's requested destination, publication and account permission changes remain separate actions.

Confirm recording and monitoring have stopped. Restore temporary settings to their recorded values where appropriate, preserving a user-requested reusable configuration. Report residual state, private failed takes and any cleanup decision rather than deleting them automatically. If recovery from a crash is needed, preserve the partial capture, use only a supported recovery/remux route on a copy, and report the actual recovered interval; do not promise missing footage can be reconstructed.

## Fictional, unexecuted example

A fictional user requests a silent 90-second recording showing how to filter a sample catalogue and export a preview. The pilot captures the main window but omits the export dialog, and the recorder's global microphone is still enabled.

The full take would be held. Within the approved capture scope, the operator would select a supported source that includes the harmless dialog, disable the unrequested audio route and review a new pilot. The final saved file would need to show both the filtered catalogue and the actual preview result, with no audio track containing room sound. A successful main-window preview alone would not satisfy acceptance.

No recorder, app, permission, video file, export or playback was operated for this fictional illustration.
