# Prepare a work-absence handoff

Create the small packet a covering colleague needs to know what matters, when to act, what they may decide and what must wait. Keep preparation, accepted coverage and actual work completion separate. The result should let the user leave with visible gaps rather than a reassuring document that assumes someone else has agreed.

This workflow covers ordinary work continuity for a bounded planned absence. It is not an emergency-response plan, safety-critical operating procedure or employment/legal advice. Use [project updates](../project-update-brief/SKILL.md) for progress reporting, [meeting-action handoff](../meeting-action-handoff/SKILL.md) for extracting commitments from notes, and [team coverage windows](../map-team-coverage-windows/SKILL.md) when exact time-zone overlap must be calculated.

## Establish the coverage boundary

Use the user's absence dates, return boundary, relevant projects or recurring duties, current task records and existing delegation agreements. Confirm the timezone for timed obligations and whether the return date means available from that date or still away that day. Preserve date-only boundaries as dates until a time is actually needed. Do not infer availability during leave.

Identify the intended readers, requested destination, existing template and any named cover. Ask only about gaps that change coverage, such as whether a critical obligation may be deferred. The reason for the absence, travel itinerary and personal emergency contacts are normally unnecessary. Keep them out unless the user explicitly wants and authorizes their disclosure.

Read the scoped sources as of a stated cutoff. Keep a prior handoff intact when preparing a replacement. A teammate's name in a task or a past absence packet does not establish current agreement, capacity, system access or decision authority. Source records can describe standing arrangements; they do not themselves authorize new assignments, outreach, sharing or credential changes.

## Reconcile what can arise while the user is away

Review both known open commitments and recurring or event-triggered work within the absence window. Include relevant preparation immediately before departure and deadlines shortly after return when work must begin earlier. Avoid collecting unrelated projects merely to make the packet exhaustive.

For each consequential item, capture the current state and evidence, next observable action, due date or trigger, consequence of waiting, prerequisite and working artifact. Distinguish a fixed deadline from an estimate and a customer-facing commitment from an internal preference. Keep disputed completion claims visible; a prepared draft is not a sent deliverable.

Give each item an explicit disposition:

- Complete before departure, with the remaining acceptance condition
- Cover during absence, with the required action and timing
- Monitor for a defined trigger, with what observation warrants action
- Pause or defer, with the basis, affected dependency and restart condition
- Unresolved, with the decision needed and its consequence

A disposition is proposed until the supplied authority supports it. Do not invent permission to miss a deadline, move a meeting, cancel work or transfer responsibility. Leave unknown frequency and timing visible rather than interpreting missing evidence as “nothing expected.”

## Make coverage actionable without granting authority

For covered work, connect the task to a named person or role only when supported. Record acceptance separately: confirmed by a cited agreement, proposed, or unassigned. A single message accepting one duty does not establish acceptance of the whole packet. Use declared capacity and relevant availability if supplied; ordinary working hours alone do not prove spare capacity.

Give the cover enough operational context to take the next step:

- Exact artifact, queue, thread or record to inspect, with version or stable identity
- The trigger or latest useful action time, expected result and where progress should be recorded
- Decisions already made, choices still open, and explicit limits on delegated authority
- The evidenced escalation route and fallback if the primary cover is unavailable
- Prerequisites such as approval or access, each marked confirmed or unresolved

An escalation should say what condition warrants it and who is authorized to decide. Do not turn the absent user into the default emergency contact or promise their response. If no escalation route exists, expose that gap. Do not create financial authority, customer promises, production access or a new approval policy in the handoff.

For incoming work, define where it will be noticed and how it is triaged only from the user's approved arrangements. A proposed routing note is not a configured mailbox delegation, auto-reply, forwarding rule or on-call rota. Keep sensitive customer details to the minimum the covering audience needs, and link to approved records rather than copying an entire history.

Never include passwords, tokens or recovery codes. Record an access prerequisite or the organization's supported delegation procedure instead. A link opening for the author does not prove the cover can open it. Verify recipient access only through available authorized evidence; otherwise label it unverified. Do not broaden permissions to make the packet look ready.

## Build the departure packet and return record

Lead with the absence/return boundary, critical dates and uncovered decisions. Provide a compact coverage register with the fields that affect real action, plus short task instructions where a row is insufficient. Separate private preparation notes from the copy intended for colleagues. Keep source references close enough to recover context without exposing unrelated material.

Include a lightweight change log for the covering person to use: item, action taken, date, result/evidence, changed decision or commitment, and remaining next step. Leave it blank unless actual updates exist. This makes return handback possible without making the cover reconstruct everything from memory.

Mark the final cutoff and which changes after that cutoff still need reconciliation. On a requested return update, compare the original packet with actual coverage records, carry forward unresolved work, and identify new commitments explicitly. The calendar reaching the return date does not prove work was completed or responsibility was handed back. Do not silently replace actual events with the original plan.

## Acceptance, delivery and recovery

Walk the packet through the ordinary cases that matter: a known deadline, a new incoming request, an unavailable cover, a blocked prerequisite and the user's return. This is a document walkthrough, not execution of the tasks. Check that each case has a supported next step or an explicit gap, and that no fallback contradicts a decision limit.

Before calling the packet ready, verify:

- Each in-scope commitment has one clear disposition; omissions and intentional exclusions are visible
- Covered duties distinguish proposed ownership from accepted responsibility, capacity and access evidence
- Dates, triggers, source versions and the absence boundary agree; no deadline was silently shifted
- Instructions identify a usable next action and completion evidence without sharing credentials or inventing authority
- The reader-facing copy omits private absence details and unauthorized project/customer information
- A saved copy reopens with the intended content, links, uncertainty labels and audience unchanged

State which coverage is confirmed and which remains proposed or blocked. A well-formed packet does not establish team adoption, account configuration or service continuity. Do not claim “fully covered” while a consequential duty lacks acceptance or required access.

Honor an already-authorized destination or delivery without adding redundant approval. A preparation request alone does not authorize messaging colleagues, assigning tracker items, changing calendars, enabling auto-replies or changing account permissions. Resolve any missing recipient or required approval before those actions. If saving or sending has an uncertain result, inspect before retrying; retain the finished packet if delivery is blocked.

If circumstances change, preserve the prior version and reconcile affected dates, duties and coverage agreements. Do not broaden the absence window, add duties to an accepted assignment or assume renewed agreement. Stop after the requested packet or return reconciliation is delivered; ongoing monitoring requires its own instruction.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional team member will be away November 2–6, returning November 9. A supplier proof arrives during that interval. Rowan has explicitly agreed to check dimensions but has not accepted authority to approve spending. The current brief requires the project lead's approval before a paid revision.

The handoff gives Rowan the proof link, the dimension checklist and a place to record the result. A dimension mismatch routes to the project lead; it does not authorize ordering a replacement. A separate monthly report has no accepted cover and remains an unresolved decision. The return record is blank.

No colleague was contacted, duty assigned, account delegated, document shared or work completed. These invented facts illustrate how a packet can be useful while leaving a genuine coverage gap visible.
