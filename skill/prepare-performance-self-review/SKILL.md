# Write a self-review that the evidence and the person support

Deliver the actual self-review in the requested form, with a private evidence map and the few questions that materially affect it. Connect what the person was asked to accomplish, what they contributed, what resulted and what they want to improve. A project status report or list of impressive activities does not answer a periodic self-assessment.

Use [prepare-role-targeted-resume](../prepare-role-targeted-resume/SKILL.md) for a job-application document, [project-update-brief](../project-update-brief/SKILL.md) for project status, and [prepare-meeting-materials](../prepare-meeting-materials/SKILL.md) for a separate conversation agenda. This workflow is for the user's own review. It does not assess other employees, recommend personnel decisions, negotiate compensation or submit to an HR system.

## Establish the period, form and voice

Read the requested review period, role and responsibilities, agreed goals or competencies, current form/prompts, word limits and intended audience. Use the user's career notes, work artifacts, prior review commitments and authorized feedback. A few supplied records can support a useful draft; do not require a complete mailbox or personnel archive.

Preserve the form's actual field labels and question meanings. Determine whether the requested result is prose, a completed editable template or private discussion notes. A draft in conversation does not authorize writing into a live HR form. If no template exists, use a simple structure that covers contributions, challenges/learning and proposed next-period priorities without inventing the employer's rubric.

Distinguish the period being reviewed from the source cutoff. A later report may establish a result that occurred within the period; a later result should not silently become an in-period achievement. Preserve changes in role, goals, resources or scope with their dates and authority. Do not judge an old commitment against a goal adopted afterward.

Ask for missing personal reflection when it changes first-person claims. Do not invent satisfaction, regret, motivation, ambition, a reason for absence or an intention to seek promotion. Supported neutral factual prose can proceed while a subjective answer remains open. Match the user's supplied voice rather than adding exaggerated praise or a performative weakness.

## Reconcile goals and contributions

Build a compact private map linking each meaningful prompt or goal to its expectation, relevant source, the user's actual contribution, result/status and remaining question. Use a few notes for a short review; a large evidence ledger is not mandatory.

Keep four layers distinct:

- **Expectation:** the agreed outcome or behavior, including changed scope and any supplied success measure
- **Contribution:** what the user personally did, decided, produced or coordinated
- **Outcome:** what the evidence shows happened, with scope, dates and any attribution limits
- **Reflection:** the user's interpretation, learning or proposed adjustment, grounded in their own account

Reconcile duplicated mentions of the same achievement across updates. Do not count drafting, approval and delivery as separate completed outcomes unless they were genuinely separate goals. Include relevant maintenance, collaboration and enabling work where supported, rather than favoring only visible launches. A lack of quantified benefit does not make the work valueless; describe its evidenced scope instead of inventing a metric.

Preserve the difference between participation and ownership, leading and supporting, team results and personal contribution. Credit collaborators by role or authorized name when needed. Do not make the user's narrative stronger by diminishing another person, guessing motives or attributing a failure to someone whose role is not established.

For every quantitative claim, retain the unit, population, period, source and comparison basis. A delivery count is not a quality improvement; a before/after change is not automatically caused by the user's work. Keep approximate figures approximate and unresolved denominators out of percentage claims. A self-reported result can be used with its actual support level; do not imply independent verification.

## Address unfinished goals and contradictory evidence

Review each relevant original and revised goal. Give it an evidence-based state such as completed for the stated scope, partly completed, ongoing, superseded, canceled or not established. A missed target should not disappear from the review simply because another achievement is easier to describe.

For a gap, distinguish the expected outcome, what actually happened, the user's contribution or decision, relevant evidenced constraints, and the remaining work. Do not convert a known missed target into an unqualified success, or turn an external constraint into an unsupported excuse. The user can acknowledge a specific action they would change without accepting responsibility for facts not established by the record.

If feedback conflicts, preserve who reported which observation and the context where disclosure is allowed. Separate a quotation from interpretation. Do not average incompatible feedback into a numerical score, infer general competence from one incident, or treat a message count as corroboration. Ask about contradictions that would change the review's central claim and complete unaffected sections.

Prior development commitments need their own check: agreed, attempted, completed and demonstrated change are different states. Attending training does not establish improved performance. Describe any actual application and remaining evidence without claiming an effect from participation alone.

## Write complete answers with appropriate boundaries

Draft each requested answer so it responds to that prompt. Select a few substantial examples with enough context to show the contribution and outcome, rather than appending every work record. Keep factual caveats in the answer when they materially change its meaning; a private appendix cannot repair an overstated headline.

Use the employer's competency language only when the example actually demonstrates the stated behavior. Do not reverse-engineer the wording merely to look like a higher rating. A supplied scale is not evidence of what rating the employer will give.

If the form requires a self-rating, preserve the user's explicit choice and its rationale. If they have not chosen, map the supplied rubric to the evidence and flag the choice for them; leave the final self-rating unresolved rather than silently selecting one or predicting the manager's decision. Do not invent comparison data about peers, eligibility for advancement or a compensation entitlement.

For challenges or development, connect a concrete observation to an actionable proposal: what practice could change, what support is needed, an opportunity to apply it, and how the user could recognize progress. Label new goals, support requests and target dates as proposed unless already agreed. Do not promise that a manager will provide coaching, budget, staffing or protected time.

The self-review can mention career interests the user actually stated. Keep a separate compensation, promotion, complaint or disciplinary message outside this routine drafting workflow unless expressly requested and handled with the authority and care that communication requires.

## Minimize private and consequential material

Keep the evidence map and personal reflection notes separate from the audience-facing review. Remove unrelated personnel information, customer details, secrets and confidential project links that the intended reader is not authorized to receive. Do not automatically attach source messages or reveal an attributed private remark because it supports a claim.

Health, accommodations, family circumstances, protected characteristics, complaints and allegations may have serious consequences if disclosed. Do not require them to explain performance or weave them into the draft without a specific user request for that content and audience. If sensitive content is necessary, surface the exact inclusion decision privately and preserve a version that omits it. Do not infer diagnoses or offer legal or medical advice.

Preserve original records and wording. When generalizing a sensitive project example, retain the true scale and nature of the contribution; anonymization is not permission to invent a more impressive story. A small audience does not make an unauthorized disclosure harmless.

## Deliver and check the exact draft

Deliver the completed audience-ready answers and a concise private note of unsupported claims, unresolved rating/personal-reflection choices and proposed next-period commitments. Identify the review period and whether the artifact is ready for the user's review or has a specific blocked field. Do not call an incomplete form submission-ready.

Before delivery:

- Check each prompt is answered or visibly held for a consequential missing input
- Trace achievement, ownership, completion and impact language to the actual evidence
- Reconcile original goals, scope changes and unfinished work without silently dropping inconvenient outcomes
- Check numbers, periods, attribution, quotations and supplied rubric terminology
- Confirm first-person feelings and goals came from the user, and proposed support has not become an accepted commitment
- Inspect the audience-facing copy for private notes, comments, unresolved placeholders and restricted links

Save a separate version when requested, preserving the source form and prior review. Reopen the actual file and verify its fields, length constraints, reading order and content. State which native-format or rendering checks were unavailable. If saving is uncertain, inspect before retrying; if another person changed the destination, reconcile rather than overwrite their work.

If new evidence changes an achievement or period boundary, update the affected answer and private support map together. Stop when the requested draft and review decisions are delivered. Sharing it, contacting a manager, completing an attestation or submitting an HR record needs separate scope and applicable approval. A polished draft establishes neither an official rating nor a career outcome.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

A fictional operations specialist had an agreed goal to publish a new handoff guide by the end of the quarter. They drafted it and incorporated team feedback; approval arrived the following quarter. During the review period they also maintained a weekly exception report. No measured time saving is supplied. Their own note says they want earlier checkpoints with the approver.

The draft says: “I drafted the handoff guide and incorporated the team's feedback during the quarter. Final approval followed after the period, so the publication goal was not completed by the original target. I also maintained the weekly exception report. For the next cycle, I propose agreeing an earlier approval checkpoint.” The private map retains the original target, approval date and the user's reflection. It does not claim publication, a percentage improvement or an agreed new process. A required self-rating remains for the user to choose.

All facts here are invented. No employee was assessed, manager contacted, document submitted, rating assigned or improvement measured.
