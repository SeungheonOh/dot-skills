# Plan the fabric layout before anything is cut

Deliver an annotated placement plan and a short piece-count check for the selected pattern and actual fabric. Show whether the required pieces fit under their real orientation and layer constraints, or identify exactly what remains unresolved. A yardage recommendation, summed piece area or attractive arrangement of silhouettes does not establish that the garment's pieces can be obtained.

Use [reconcile-design-cut-list](../reconcile-design-cut-list/SKILL.md) for a general material-demand ledger. This workflow owns sewing-specific placement: one folded outline may produce one complete piece, two fabric layers may produce a mirrored pair, and a space-saving rotation may violate the pattern or fabric direction. It does not alter garment fit, add seams, grade sizes, provide a construction tutorial or authorize physical cutting.

## Establish the pattern and fabric being planned

Start from the selected pattern edition, view, size and already approved alterations. Obtain the instructions and full-size pieces, not just the miniature layout illustration. Record:

- Required pieces for the main fabric, lining, interfacing and any contrast fabric separately
- Each piece's cut quantity, grainline, fold edge, face/handedness and included seam or hem allowances
- Actual fabric length and usable width, selvages, relevant defects and the state in which those measurements were taken
- Nap, directional print, stretch-direction requirements and any chosen motif or seam-matching priorities
- Whether the requested output is a placement sketch, a dimensioned digital layout or a review of an existing layout

Ask only for facts that change feasibility. If actual outlines or scale are unavailable, return a provisional requirement review rather than inventing silhouettes or declaring that the pieces fit. Keep the user's size and material choice; a shortage is not permission to resize the garment or substitute fabric.

Plan with the fabric dimensions expected at cutting. If measurements precede required preparation and shrinkage is unknown, make the fit conclusion conditional on remeasurement; do not deduct a guessed universal shrink percentage. Separate usable cloth from nominal bolt width and exclude reported damaged or unusable regions. These are supplied observations, not a remote inspection of fabric quality.

## 1. Verify the selected shapes before arranging them

Read the actual pattern's allowance convention. Some patterns include allowances and others do not; never add a second allowance because it seems customary. Seamwork's [allowance guidance](https://help.seamwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004183634-Are-seam-allowances-included-in-the-patterns) is an example of a publisher-specific rule with exceptions in the individual instructions, not a rule for all patterns. If necessary allowances are absent or unclear, hold the affected placement until the correct cutting outline is established.

For tiled PDF pieces, reconcile page joins, selected size lines and any extension pieces. Preserve notches, match points and grain marks in the working copy. A miniature cutting diagram is not a scale template. For printed output, inspect the publisher's calibration shape before trusting dimensions; [Seamwork's PDF instructions](https://help.seamwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004181994-How-to-download-and-print-PDF-patterns) distinguish actual-size printing from scale-to-fit. Digital dimensions alone do not establish the printed size.

Use unchanged source outlines or clearly identified working copies. Do not rotate the grainline independently of its piece, smooth a curve to improve packing, or redistribute a licensed pattern as part of a public layout example. A private planning image need not reproduce every construction page.

## 2. Count fabric pieces, not paper templates

Make a compact occurrence list before placement. For each material, expand what the pattern actually requests: one piece, a mirrored pair, two identical pieces, or one full piece obtained on a fold. Keep left/right or asymmetric components distinct. A label such as "cut two" must be interpreted with that pattern's instructions rather than treated as an unrestricted duplicate operation.

Record the layer arrangement beside each placement:

- A half-pattern placed exactly on its specified fold yields one continuous full piece when opened, not two separate pieces.
- A placement through two appropriately oriented layers can supply a mirrored pair. It must not also be counted as two additional single-layer placements.
- On a single layer, a mirrored pair needs the corresponding reversed template orientation. Turning a piece end-for-end is not the same transformation as flipping it.
- Lining and interfacing requirements remain separate even when they reuse the same paper shape.

The University of Kentucky's [layout guide](https://fcs.mgcafe.uky.edu/sites/fcs.mgcafe.uky.edu/files/ct-mmb-181.pdf) explains single/double thickness, exact fold placement and the risk of obtaining two same-side pieces from a single-layer layout. Preserve the instructions for asymmetric designs rather than forcing every pair into a generic mirroring rule.

If a fold piece must be planned on a single layer, use its complete unfolded shape only when that expansion is established and permitted by the pattern. Do not place the half-template once on flat cloth or introduce a center seam as a silent substitute. Label the full shape and keep the original fold reference.

## 3. Treat grain and fabric direction as placement constraints

Start from the publisher's layout for the selected view, size and fabric width. Adapt it only where the actual piece and material rules permit. Keep every grainline's required relationship to the fabric; a narrow gap is not permission to turn a straight-grain piece onto crossgrain or bias. A specified bias placement remains bias rather than being "corrected" to lengthwise grain.

For a straight grainline intended parallel to a known straight selvage or fold, compare the distance from both ends of the marked line to that reference. Equal distances establish parallel placement, not material suitability. [Seamwork's cutting guidance](https://www.seamwork.com/sewing-tutorials/cutting-and-marking-fabric) also explains why pile, shading and one-way prints can require a common direction even when a 180-degree rotation preserves parallel grain.

Label the fabric's chosen top/nap direction, right side and layer orientation. Do not infer the correct visible direction solely from an arrow in a cropped photo. For directional material, check every occurrence, including the lower layer of a folded arrangement. If the supplied fold arrangement would reverse a required direction between layers, retain that conflict and use a supported alternate arrangement rather than declaring the pair equivalent.

Keep permitted flips and rotations explicit. A shape may fit geometrically while showing its wrong face, reversing a motif or violating stretch direction. These failures cannot be repaired by reporting a smaller fabric length.

## 4. Resolve selected print matches before claiming a fit

When matching is requested, identify which seams, pockets or motifs matter to the user. Do not promise that every stripe can match across a shaped garment. Record the relevant repeat and reference points from the actual fabric, including whether the design is directional or uneven. A repeat distance alone does not locate the first usable motif.

Match the intended sewn relationship, not just adjacent cut edges. The Sewing & Craft Alliance's [stripe guide](https://www.sewing.org/files/guidelines/11_260_working_with_stripes.pdf) locates matching at seamlines and notes that uneven stripes can require a one-way layout. Preserve allowance distances and match marks when comparing adjoining pieces. Two matching raw edges can cease to match after the allowances are sewn.

Use an explicit placement choice for each required match. [Seamwork's pattern-matching guide](https://www.seamwork.com/sewing-tutorials/how-to-match-plaids-stripes-and-large-patterns) distinguishes horizontal and vertical repeats and prioritizes visible matches. Do not add a fixed extra-yardage percentage and treat it as a demonstrated layout. If the motif cannot be mapped from the available evidence, retain grain/count planning and mark the matching-dependent fit unresolved.

## 5. Check a complete layout against real fabric regions

Place the most constrained pieces first, then fit remaining pieces without relaxing their rules. Use actual cutting outlines when available. Bounding rectangles may demonstrate a conservative arrangement if every outline lies inside them, but a failed rectangle arrangement does not prove the real shapes cannot fit. Summed area is only a lower bound, not a placement plan or an optimality claim.

Show usable fabric boundaries, folds, selvages, layer count, direction and each piece occurrence. Distinguish sequential layouts that use separate cloth regions from alternate proposals that reuse the same region. If a plan unfolds or refolds a remnant, track that remnant back to the original fabric so material already assigned elsewhere is not counted again.

Review the whole arrangement before presenting a length requirement:

1. Every required fabric piece is produced once, with its correct face, handedness and material.
2. Outlines stay inside usable cloth, do not overlap on the same layer and avoid excluded regions.
3. Fold edges, grain, nap/stretch direction and selected motif matches all remain satisfied.
4. Any needed separation or preparation space follows the actual method or supplied rule, not an invented universal margin.
5. The proposed length covers the complete arrangement; allowances, shrinkage or matching extras have not been silently omitted or counted twice.

If no acceptable arrangement is found, distinguish "this layout does not fit" from "no valid layout exists." Name the blocking piece or constraint. Offer a changed layout within existing permissions, or ask about more fabric or an explicit design/matching change. Do not shorten a piece, omit a lining or add piecing merely to obtain a pass.

## Deliver the plan and its limits

Return the placement plan, piece/layer count check, fabric dimensions and material-specific holds. A small project can use one annotated page. Keep the planning diagram separate from an actual-size cutting template; label a reduced preview as not to scale.

Read back the saved plan and check that its labels, dimensions and occurrences agree with the final arrangement. Distinguish source-document review, digital geometry review, measured paper layout, actual cloth layout and physical cutting. Do not claim the user has enough usable cloth from a screenshot without scale or that the completed garment will fit from a cutting layout alone.

Stop when the requested plan is supported at the available evidence level, or the specific missing measurement or pattern instruction has been identified. No fabric purchase, physical marking/cutting, pattern alteration or external sharing follows automatically from preparation.

## Fictional, unexecuted illustration

An invented accessory pattern requires one body on a fold and one mirrored pair of side panels in directional main fabric. A proposed single-layer layout places the half-body once and two identical face-up side templates. Its three visible shapes fit within the drawn fabric rectangle, but it does not produce the required three fabric pieces: the body is incomplete and the sides have the same handedness.

The revised proposal must use an established full-body outline on the single layer, or a suitable fold arrangement, and reverse the correct side-panel template while retaining the required motif direction. Only then can its placement extent support a fabric-length claim. If the full body does not fit, the unused total area is not evidence that it can be made without an authorized change.

This example is original planning reasoning. No pattern was generated, layout solver run, fabric measured or cut, garment sewn, application tested or purchase made.
